# Coty Inc. — Q1 2027 (Q&A) (2026-11-05)

## 요약(stockanalysis 자동 생성)

### Executive summary

- Q1 came ahead of expectations but remained below management’s ambitions; FY27 is a transition year with a non-linear recovery. Strategic review targeted for completion by calendar year-end 2026.
- Top six core Prestige brands grew 5% like-for-like; management expects growth from them in Q2 and plans continued brand investment, cash generation and debt reduction.


### Financial highlights

- Gross margin declined 120 bps; roughly 50 bps reflected Middle East freight and material costs, with the balance driven by unfavorable mix and excess and obsolete inventory.
- Q1 performance was ahead of expectations, while management emphasized that results still need to improve.


### Outlook and guidance

- Top six core Prestige brands are expected to grow in Q2; broader recovery is expected to be non-linear during the FY27 transition year.
- Strategic review is due by calendar year-end 2026; savings and restructuring plans to address Gucci’s lost contribution are expected before Christmas, with plans finalized no later than January.
- Middle East cost headwinds are expected to persist through the rest of the year; oil-related input costs are forecast at $30m-$40m, at the upper end if oil stays above $100/barrel.
- Productivity across the P&L, fixed-cost actions and marketing ROI thresholds are intended to offset cost pressure while preserving investment in core brands.


### Segment performance

- Top six core Prestige brands grew 5%; excluding Marc Jacobs Beauty pipeline fill, Prestige would still have grown, with the top six nearer 3%.
- Consumer decline narrowed from 8% in Q1 FY26 to 3% in Q1 FY27; CoverGirl net sales declined 2% this quarter versus nearly 8% in H1 FY26.
- Asia net sales rose 8%, with China posting a particularly strong quarter; Europe remains a focus, with softer Prestige markets in Germany and France and growth in Spain and Italy.
- Skincare profitability improved; management said performance across Prestige, Consumer and skincare supports future strategic optionality.


### Key financial ratios and metrics

- Gross margin fell 120 bps in Q1; Middle East freight and material costs accounted for about 50 bps of the decline.
- Gucci represented a double-digit share of the portfolio, with gross margin around 70%, A&CP around 29% and profitability in line with Prestige; its exit impact is sizable.


### Risk factors and uncertainties

- Excess and obsolete inventory, unfavorable mix and remnants of scattered, smaller innovations weighed on gross margin; inventory cleanup and improved planning will take time.
- Middle East freight, material and oil-related input costs remain a headwind; the forecast impact could reach its upper range if oil remains above $100/barrel.
- Gucci’s exit creates a sizable profitability impact; exact transition timing is still being determined, and savings and restructuring plans are being developed to address it.
- Europe’s lower market growth, particularly in Prestige in Germany and France, and continued macro pressure could challenge performance and costs.


### Significant events and developments

- Strategic review remains underway, with completion targeted by the end of calendar 2026; portfolio decisions are not to be inferred from Q1 brand results.
- Four larger innovations launched in Q1—BOSS Bottled Beyond for Her, Mood Stones by Kylie, Marc Jacobs Beauty and Burberry Goddess Amber Vanilla—were tracking ahead of objectives.
- Marc Jacobs Beauty launch approach included retailer co-development with Sephora, a tighter SKU range and influencer- and creator-led advocacy; management plans to apply these practices to other makeup brands.
- Brandlight partnership will focus AI-search efforts on the top six brands in five markets, prioritizing depth by brand and market over broad initial coverage.
- Marc Jacobs license was extended; management favors extending licenses early and for longer periods to support investment security.


### Capital allocation and financing

- Gucci agreement totals $400m, including $250m received upfront; proceeds compensate for one fewer year of profit, cash flow and inventory and fund restructuring costs.
- The agreement is intended to protect FY28 cash flow; continued investment behind core brands and debt reduction remain priorities.
- Incremental investment is concentrated in advocacy, including influencer and creator activity; productivity actions are intended to preserve brand investment.


### Market and industry conditions

- Global fragrance category growth was 5% in Q1, with ongoing competition and investment needs supporting management’s preference for longer license terms.
- U.S. retailers are starting holiday activity earlier than last year, shifting activity earlier in the quarter.
- China was particularly strong and total Asia net sales rose 8%; European Prestige market growth was softer in Germany and France than in Spain and Italy.
- Management described fragrance and makeup as increasingly driven by influencer and creator advocacy, and is directing investment toward these capabilities.
