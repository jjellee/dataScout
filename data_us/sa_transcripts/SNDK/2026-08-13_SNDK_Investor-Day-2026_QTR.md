# Sandisk Corporation — Investor Day 2026 (2026-08-13)

## 요약(stockanalysis 자동 생성)

### Strategic vision and business transformation

- Restructured business model to focus on long-term, multi-year customer agreements, shifting away from volatile, quarterly pricing cycles to improve visibility and reduce cyclicality.
- Data center established as a major growth pillar, with significant share gains and outsized growth expected to continue through FY 2027.
- Consumer business rebranded and expanded, gaining global market share and serving as a financial shock absorber during industry downturns.
- Extended joint venture with Kioxia to secure NAND supply through 2034 and invested in DRAM access to support enterprise SSD contracts.
- Capital allocation prioritizes investment in technology, maintaining a debt-free balance sheet, and returning excess cash to shareholders via buybacks.


### Technology and innovation roadmap

- Accelerated NAND innovation with 19 generations delivered, recently adding BiCS9 and BiCS10, and ongoing work on BiCS11 and beyond.
- Introduced hybrid bonding (CBA) technology, enabling rapid performance upgrades with minimal capital, supporting fast transitions in data center interfaces.
- BiCS10 achieves over 60% density improvement and 75% better power efficiency versus BiCS8, with sampling to customers underway.
- High-Bandwidth Flash (HBF) positioned to address AI memory bottlenecks, with first die taped out and customer sampling planned for next year.
- 3D Matrix Memory and HBF for Edge under development, targeting both data center and edge AI applications.


### Market outlook and financial guidance

- Flash market projected to reach $300B in 2026 and $500B in 2027, driven by data center demand and AI adoption.
- Data center share of total addressable market (TAM) has risen from 20% to 50% in recent years, with edge shifting to premium devices.
- Committed to mid-to-high teens bit and revenue growth through 2030, with non-GAAP gross margin targeted at ~80% and operating margin at 75%.
- Free cash flow run rate at $20B, with 100% of excess cash returned to shareholders; $15.5B in buyback authorization remains.
- Eight major NVM contracts signed, averaging four years in duration, covering over $91B in remaining performance obligations and $16.5B in financial guarantees.


### AI infrastructure and product positioning

- Flash is critical across all AI infrastructure layers, especially for persistent KV cache, projected to be 35% of the flash market by 2030.
- AI data center workloads drive demand for both TLC and QLC SSDs, with products qualified and shipping at major hyperscalers.
- Internal AI Lab validates flash's efficiency, showing 75% less energy use and higher throughput versus volatile memory-only systems.
- HBF and persistent KV cache expected to unlock new AI use cases, enabling memory-centric AI and edge AI models with long context and high capacity.
- Open ecosystem for HBF established, with industry partners and advisory board guiding future standards and adoption.


### Risk management and future outlook

- Business model changes and multi-year contracts aim to dampen industry cyclicality and provide long-term demand visibility.
- Technology and manufacturing scalability ensure ability to meet surges in demand without overproducing.
- Ongoing focus on customer diversity, maintaining robust edge and consumer portfolios alongside data center growth.
- Management confident in ability to adapt to market shifts, with innovation and customer co-development at the core of strategy.
- Skepticism acknowledged, but leadership emphasizes structural changes and strategic execution as drivers of sustainable value creation.

A key step is creating embeddings, which turn content into numeric vectors so computers can group and search by meaning, not just keywords. And for AI training, the system has to pull data randomly from across the entire enriched data set fast and at a very high throughput. Stage 3 is model training. Massive GPU clusters process that prepared data to learn patterns and build a model. Training can run for a long time, so the system regularly saves progress in checkpoints. If something fails, you can restart from the last known good snapshot instead of losing days of work. Because training pauses during checkpointing, the faster those snapshots write and store, the more time the GPUs spend learning. Stage 4 is inference, when trained models are actually used to answer questions and generate outputs. Real services may swap models depending on the task, so keeping the right models ready matters. As more tokens are generated with agentic workflows, the more valuable this cache becomes, and the larger it becomes. These high-capacity KV caches are tiered onto SSDs. Stage 5 is new content generation. The model's outputs are stored immediately so they can be delivered to users, and reused for things like fine-tuning and ongoing consumption.
