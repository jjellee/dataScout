# Core Scientific, Inc. — TD Cowen's 54th Annual Technology, Media & Telecom Conference (2026-05-28)

## 요약(stockanalysis 자동 생성)

### Strategic priorities and expansion

- Focused on expanding five new sites, supported by a recent capital raise that pre-funded significant equity for projects.
- Aggressively purchasing land and securing power for a three-year development pipeline, with first RFS in 2027.
- Diversifying the customer base and deploying capital to position for long-term contracts.
- Planning for future requirements, including 800V buildings for 2028 deliveries.


### Market demand and customer requirements

- Notable shift in hyperscaler demand, with structural increases and evolving requirements for higher densities and new voltage standards.
- Customers increasingly require flexibility in design to accommodate rapid changes.
- Shift in hyperscaler willingness to allow over 50% behind-the-meter power, driving land and engineering needs.


### Site development and operational learnings

- Pecos and Muskogee sites highlighted for their scale and expansion potential, with unique labor and infrastructure challenges addressed.
- Labor market remains highly competitive, with labor costs rising 20-30% and now comprising up to 40% of build costs.
- Key learnings include the importance of flexible design, strong national labor partners, and proactive supply chain management.


### Financial strategy and deal structuring

- Hardline requirement for credit backstops with non-investment grade customers, often involving chipmakers or other investment-grade entities.
- Yields for hyperscale deals expected in the 12-14% range, with potential for higher returns in neocloud deals by picking winners.
- Hybrid deals (part turnkey, part powered shell) can offer attractive yields and reduced development risk, but current focus is on full delivery GPU-ready facilities.


### Build costs, supply chain, and inflation

- Build costs have risen from $8M to $11.5-12M per MW, mainly due to labor inflation and supply chain constraints.
- Anticipated design changes (e.g., 800V, stripping out UPSs) may reduce costs to around $10M per MW in coming years.
- Longest lead times are for medium voltage switchgear (up to 100 weeks), prompting proactive pre-buying.


### Industry outlook and consolidation

- Expectation of industry consolidation as valuations normalize and execution becomes a key differentiator.
- Focus remains on scaling existing sites and signing large customers to drive organic growth and position for future consolidation opportunities.


### Community engagement and challenges

- Increasing public backlash against data centers, requiring proactive community engagement and transparency.
- Emphasis on building trust with local communities, learning from past experiences in Bitcoin mining.


### Future focus and business model evolution

- Considering enterprise opportunities and REIT conversion in the long term, but current focus is on large-scale, single-tenant sites.
- Monitoring market trends for potential acquisition of smaller enterprise-focused operators.
