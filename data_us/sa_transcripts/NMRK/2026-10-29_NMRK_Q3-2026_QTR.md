# Newmark Group, Inc. — Q3 2026 (2026-10-29)

## 요약(stockanalysis 자동 생성)

### Executive summary

- Record Q3 revenue of $973.2M, up 12.7% YoY; adjusted EBITDA rose 14.6% and adjusted EPS rose 14.3%.
- Management and servicing grew 18.3%, while Leasing rose 26.5%; Capital Markets revenue declined 4.4%.
- Active pipelines and resilient recurring businesses support growth, but macro volatility and slower deal closings led to revised 2026 guidance.


### Financial highlights

- Revenue reached a third-quarter record $973.2M, versus $863.5M a year earlier.
- Adjusted EBITDA increased 14.6% to $166.4M from $145.2M; adjusted EPS rose 14.3% to $0.48 from $0.42.
- Total expenses increased 13.5%; excluding pass-through items, expenses rose 10.8%, largely due to commission-based revenue and global growth initiatives.


### Outlook and guidance

- FY26 revenue guidance is $3.7B-$3.8B, up 12%-15%; adjusted EBITDA is $629M-$667M, up 12%-19%.
- FY26 adjusted EPS guidance is $1.80-$1.90, up 11%-17%; adjusted earnings tax rate is 13%-15%, versus 11.4% a year earlier.
- FY26 results are expected to set records for revenue, adjusted EBITDA, and adjusted EPS; guidance excludes future acquisitions and assumes no meaningful share-price change.
- For 2027, recurring businesses are expected to grow double digits and Leasing at a slower, single-digit pace; earnings are currently viewed within ±10% of record 2026, excluding acquisitions.
- Capital Markets volumes are expected to remain down through at least H1 2027; a sustained rate and Treasury volatility scenario could drive Capital Markets down 20%-30%.


### Segment performance

- Management Services, Servicing Fees, and Other revenue rose 18.3%, driven by organic outsourcing growth, Valuation & Advisory improvement, and six acquisitions since Q3 last year.
- Leasing revenue rose 26.5%, led by industrial and office fees in West Coast markets and Texas; demand included technology, manufacturing, legal, aerospace, defense, and healthcare sectors.
- Capital Markets revenue fell 4.4%; investment sales volumes increased 33.4%, offset by lower originations and a difficult comparison with prior-year debt-volume growth.
- Investment sales growth was led by senior and affordable housing, with higher industrial and retail volumes; data-center leasing activity is ramping up.


### Key financial ratios and metrics

- Adjusted earnings tax rate was 12.9%, versus 12.1% in Q3 2025; fully diluted weighted average shares were 252.7M, up 0.4% YoY.
- Quarter-end cash and cash equivalents were $240M, corporate debt was $877.5M, and net leverage was 1x.
- Trailing-12-month adjusted free cash flow increased 16% to $336.8M; Q3 free cash flow was down about 10% excluding the prior-year $42M settlement benefit, described as timing-related.


### Risk factors and uncertainties

- Higher rates and volatility delayed deal closings, raised cap rates, pressured asset values, and increased uncertainty around transaction timing and volumes.
- Election uncertainty and community opposition are delaying some data-center projects, although the pipeline remains robust and no projects were said to have withdrawn.
- GSE industry activity declined about 30% in Q3; activity shifted toward non-GSE multifamily debt, and GSEs were not expected to reach annual caps in 2026.
- Large institutional portfolio and M&A deals remain active but can be lumpy and take longer to close in volatile markets.


### Significant events and developments

- CEO Barry Gosin plans to step down at year-end after 47 years; internal and external candidates are under evaluation, with a choice expected within 60 days.
- U.S. commercial and multifamily origination share rose from under 2% in 2014 to around 10%; investment sales ranking improved from No. 10 in 2014 to No. 2 for 9M 2026.
- Recurring businesses grew from 24% of revenue in 2017 to around 40% year-to-date; management and servicing are expected to exceed $2B annual revenue in 2029.
- International revenue is approximately 13% of the top line; further global expansion is anticipated over the next 18-24 months.


### Capital allocation and financing

- Repurchased 3.9M shares for $59.4M late in Q3; the board reauthorized the $400M buyback program on October 28, 2026, with further repurchases anticipated.
- Capital allocation has shifted toward M&A in recurring-revenue businesses after building out the U.S. platform; six acquisitions were completed in nearly a year, while talent hiring continues.
- Employee loan issuance fell 36.9% in 9M 2026 YoY, supporting improved cash-flow metrics; the trend is expected to continue.
- Investment in Management Services, cross-selling, and global platform growth will continue despite potential Capital Markets weakness.


### Market and industry conditions

- Management expects recurring-business fundamentals to remain strong and Leasing volumes to improve in 2027, with single-digit gains after strong double-digit growth in 2026.
- Rising rates and a higher 10-year Treasury are slowing Capital Markets activity; buyers and sellers are still working toward price discovery, unlike the prolonged uncertainty of 2023.
- Class A office vacancy is in the single digits across major markets, but limited suitable space may temper Leasing growth; conversions of B buildings to A space are increasing.
- Management sees further opportunity in powered land and data-center leasing, while GSE multifamily activity may pick up in 2027 under mandates to increase volume.
