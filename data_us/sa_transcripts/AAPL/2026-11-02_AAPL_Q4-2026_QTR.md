# Apple Inc. — Q4 2026 (2026-11-02)

## 요약(stockanalysis 자동 생성)

### Executive summary

- September-quarter revenue reached a record $114.5B, up 12% YoY; fiscal-year revenue rose 15% to a record $478.8B, with record EPS of $8.98, up 20%.
- iPhone and Services set quarterly records, while Mac grew 24%; iPad and Wearables, Home, and Accessories declined amid supply limits and difficult comparisons.
- New product launches and Siri AI expansion supported optimism, while higher memory costs, constrained supply, and FX remain key near-term pressures.


### Financial highlights

- Products revenue was $82.9B, up 12% YoY; Services revenue was $31.6B, up 10% and an all-time record.
- Net income was $30.7B; diluted EPS was $2.10, up 14% YoY. Both were September-quarter records excluding the tariff-refund benefit; EPS included $0.05 from refunds.
- Operating cash flow was $28.1B; operating expenses were $19.2B, up 21% YoY, reflecting continued R&D investment.


### Outlook and guidance

- December-quarter revenue is expected to grow 7%–10% YoY and reach a company-record level; outlook assumes current tariff rates and policies and no worsening in the global macro outlook.
- Services revenue is expected to grow 8%–9% YoY, with sequential headwinds from third-party licensing and FX.
- Gross margin is expected at 45%–46%; operating expenses at $20.4B–$20.8B, with OpEx growth of 11%–13% YoY, driven by R&D and AI investment.
- OI&E is expected near $300M, excluding potential mark-to-market effects on minority investments; tax rate is expected near 17%.


### Segment performance

- iPhone revenue was $57.6B, up 17% YoY, despite supply constraints and FX headwinds; records were set in the U.S. and Greater China.
- Mac revenue was $10.8B, up 24% YoY despite supply constraints; fiscal-year sales reached a record, with strong emerging-market performance.
- iPad revenue was $6.2B, down 10% YoY, reflecting supply constraints and a difficult comparison with the prior-year A16 iPad launch.
- Wearables, Home, and Accessories revenue was $8.3B, down 8% YoY, against prior-year Watch SE 3 and AirPods Pro 3 launches.
- Services revenue was $31.6B, up 10% YoY; video, cloud, payment services, and advertising set records, while Music reached a September-quarter record.


### Key financial ratios and metrics

- Company gross margin was 47.9% and Products gross margin was 37.2%; both included a favorable tariff-refund impact of ~1 percentage point.
- Excluding tariff refunds in both June and September quarters, Company and Products gross margin each declined ~1 point sequentially, primarily due to higher memory costs.
- Services gross margin was 76%, up 40 bps sequentially.
- Fiscal-year diluted EPS reached a record $8.98, up 20% YoY; September-quarter diluted EPS was $2.10, up 14% YoY.


### Risk factors and uncertainties

- Supply constraints affected iPhone, Mac, and iPad; December-quarter Mac and iPad constraints are expected to persist, primarily due to memory availability and demand exceeding expectations.
- Memory pricing was described as unprecedented and expected to rise further; carry-in inventory benefits are expected to diminish over time.
- FX, tariffs, macroeconomic conditions, and legal and regulatory proceedings may affect results; services also face licensing, mobile gaming, and App Store pressures.
- Server-side Siri AI use may add inference costs, and the early-stage on-device versus Private Cloud Compute mix remains difficult to predict.


### Significant events and developments

- Product launches included iPhone Duo and iPhone 18 Pro, touch-enabled MacBook Pro, OLED iPad mini, AirPods 5, and new HomeView, Apple TV 4K, and HomePod mini products.
- Siri AI launched in U.S. English beta in September and expanded to French, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese, and Spanish in October; expanded server-side access may be offered for purchase in the future.
- The active-device installed base exceeded 2.5B; paid subscriptions exceeded 1.5B and grew at a double-digit rate during the quarter.
- A Philippines Department of Education deal will deploy 170,000 MacBook Neos to teachers and staff.
- A four-year $600B U.S. innovation and advanced-manufacturing commitment includes expanded supplier partnerships and domestic component production.


### Capital allocation and financing

- Quarter-end cash and marketable securities were $137B; total debt was $80B.
- Returned $28B to shareholders during the quarter: $3.9B in dividends and equivalents and $24B in share repurchases.
- The board declared a $0.27-per-share cash dividend, payable November 16, 2026, to shareholders of record November 12, 2026.
- Net cash neutrality is no longer a target; capital allocation remains focused on funding business needs and returning excess cash, with cash and debt managed independently for flexibility.
- Manufacturing purchase obligations increased $33B from June to September as supply efforts continued.


### Notes to the financial statements

- Siri AI cloud costs will be recognized in Services cost of sales; a portion of device revenue is deferred and recognized over device life, with hardware deferrals incorporating Siri AI.
- Tariff refunds favorably affected September-quarter gross margin by ~1 percentage point and diluted EPS by $0.05; the vast majority of tariff-related refunds had been received by quarter-end.


### Market and industry conditions

- September-quarter revenue records were set in the Americas, Europe, Greater China, and the rest of Asia Pacific; India also reached an all-time record.
- Greater China revenue rose 21% YoY in the quarter and 28% for FY 2026, driven primarily by iPhone and strong Mac growth.
- September-quarter FX headwinds were nearly 2 percentage points for total revenue; Services faced over 1.5 points of sequential FX headwinds, with further sequential pressure expected in December.
- Advanced-node constraints eased as more products moved to 2-nanometer technology; memory-driven Mac and iPad constraints remain as demand exceeded expectations.
- Higher memory costs and recent product price increases are affecting margin and pricing decisions; price elasticity remains too early to assess.
