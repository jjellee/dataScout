# D-Wave Quantum Inc. — Q3 2026 (2026-11-10)

## 요약(stockanalysis 자동 생성)

### Executive summary

- Q3 revenue was $4.5M, up 21% YoY; 9M bookings reached $37.4M, up 611% YoY, with pipeline growth and customer deployments supporting commercial momentum.
- Strategy spans commercial annealing systems and QCaaS today while advancing a fault-tolerant gate model roadmap.


### Financial highlights

- Q3 revenue of $4.5M grew 21% YoY; more than 100 customers contributed, with commercial enterprises at 52% of revenue vs. 31% in Q3 2025.
- GAAP gross profit was $2.4M, down 11% YoY; non-GAAP gross profit was $2.9M, essentially flat YoY.
- GAAP operating expenses were $55.9M, up 84% YoY; non-GAAP adjusted operating expenses were $40.5M, up 72%.
- Net loss was $48.2M, or $0.13/share; YoY loss narrowed by $91.8M, primarily due to $122M less in prior-year non-cash warrant-liability remeasurement charges.
- Adjusted EBITDA loss was $37.7M, up $17M YoY, primarily reflecting higher spending on product development and go-to-market initiatives.


### Outlook and guidance

- Management expects system sales, QCaaS, professional services and partner sales to grow in 2027 vs. expected 2026 levels; system sales may represent a larger bookings and revenue mix.
- Government business is expected to become a significantly larger component; partner contributions are expected to develop in 2027 but are not yet a significant business component.
- A substantial portion of the $20M Florida Atlantic University system-sale revenue is expected in Q4 2026; installation-related revenue is likely in early 2027.
- Gate model roadmap targets a 17-qubit system and first fault-tolerant logical qubit by 2028, then 100 logical qubits capable of more than 1M operations by 2032.


### Segment performance

- QCaaS subscription revenue was $2.2M, up 56% YoY; professional services revenue was $2.0M, up 318%, driven mainly by a Fortune 100 engagement and gate-model research projects.
- Systems and other revenue was approximately $360K; a $20M Florida Atlantic University system sale was included in 9M bookings.
- NTT DOCOMO deployed a second production application; both applications were developed by NTT DOCOMO and run relatively infrequently while targeting operational and financial benefits.
- Stride solver upgrades found better solutions in 15 seconds on assembly-line-balancing benchmarks vs. 120 seconds for the prior version.
- SkyWater’s annealing-based maintenance-scheduling proof of technology reduced lot delays by up to 30%; work is advancing to a proof of concept.


### Key financial ratios and metrics

- GAAP gross margin was 52.9%, down 18.5% YoY; non-GAAP gross margin was 63.6%, down 14.1% YoY, with mix affected by lower-margin professional services.
- 9M bookings increased 611% YoY to $37.4M; Q3 bookings were $1.9M, down 19% YoY, with quarterly timing of larger transactions driving variability.
- Remaining performance obligations were $38.3M at Sept. 30, 2026, more than 13x the prior-year balance; 59% is expected to convert within 12 months and 71% within 24 months.
- Cash and marketable securities totaled $503.4M at Sept. 30, 2026, down 40% YoY, primarily reflecting approximately $253M used to acquire Quantum Circuits in January 2026.


### Risk factors and uncertainties

- Dilution refrigerators have longer-than-desired lead times; additional suppliers, including Maybell, are being qualified, and advance purchases are beginning for some long-lead items.
- Manufacturing supply-chain diversification remains a priority; the extended SkyWater relationship supports continuity and IP protection, while additional sources are being qualified.
- CHIPS Act funding of up to $100M is subject to specified milestones; no funds had been received by Q3 end.
- Gross margin may fluctuate with product mix, and quarterly bookings can vary significantly with timing of larger transactions.


### Significant events and developments

- Signed a definitive agreement with the U.S. Department of Commerce for access to up to $100M in CHIPS and Science Act funding for next-generation annealing and gate-model systems.
- Delivered Italy’s first annealing quantum computer, an Advantage2 system, for installation at Villa Saporiti; Swiss Quantum Technology SA funded the system.
- CGI plans to integrate the platforms into its advanced technology services; an MOU with LG CNS will focus on enterprise optimization.
- A gate-model simulator beta launched with commercial and research participants; discussions are underway for 17-qubit system orders for 2027 with existing and new customers.
- Completed a dual-chip annealing processor design for fabrication and remain on track to complete scalable-I/O development wafers in November, supporting longer-term 20,000- and 100,000-qubit targets.


### Capital allocation and financing

- Cash and marketable securities were $503.4M at Q3 end; approximately $253M was used for the Quantum Circuits acquisition in January 2026.
- Up to $100M of CHIPS Act funding is available subject to milestones and is intended to support next-generation annealing and gate-model development; no proceeds had been received by quarter-end.
- A multi-year SkyWater agreement covers semiconductor process development and manufacturing; additional manufacturing sources will continue to be qualified.


### Notes to the financial statements

- System-sale revenue is recognized substantially upon delivery, with additional revenue over installation, typically several months later; FAU installation revenue is expected in early 2027, followed by smaller multi-year maintenance and QCaaS streams.
- The YoY net-loss comparison reflects a $122M decline in non-cash, non-operating warrant-liability remeasurement charges; remaining publicly traded warrants were redeemed in November 2025.


### Market and industry conditions

- Gartner named the business a market shaper in its emerging-market quadrant for established quantum-computing vendors; it was one of six vendors in the upper-right quadrant.
- Sovereign supply-chain priorities vary by region: EU quantum funding is restricted to EU companies, while Asian discussions have focused on incorporating local suppliers; management expects supply chains to remain global.
- Management reported continued pipeline and sales-agreement momentum following U.S. elections, and increased U.S. government interest in annealing applications.
