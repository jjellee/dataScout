# Astronics Corporation — Q1 2026 (2026-05-12)

## 요약(stockanalysis 자동 생성)

### Executive summary

- Q1 2026 revenue reached $231 million, up 12% year-over-year, at the high end of guidance and the second-highest quarterly total ever.
- Adjusted EBITDA margin improved to 16.4% from 14.9% year-over-year, reflecting operational improvements.
- Bookings hit a record $290 million, driving backlog to an all-time high of $734 million.
- Raised full-year revenue guidance to $970 million–$1 billion, a 14–16% increase over 2025.
- No impact observed from global geopolitical conflicts, including the Iran War.


### Financial highlights

- Gross profit rose to $75 million (32.6% margin), up from $61 million (29.5%) last year, aided by higher volume and a $2.8 million MV-75 program adjustment.
- Adjusted operating income was $29.6 million (12.8% margin), up from $22.7 million (11%).
- Net income was $25.5 million ($0.67 per diluted share), up from $9.5 million ($0.26) year-over-year.
- Adjusted net income was $22.5 million, up 32.6% year-over-year; adjusted EPS was $0.59, up from $0.44.
- Adjusted EBITDA reached $37.9 million, up 23.3% year-over-year.
- Interest expense fell 25.8% to $2.3 million due to refinancing.
- Tax benefit of $800,000 recorded, with a significant one-time benefit expected upon valuation allowance release.


### Outlook and guidance

- Full-year 2026 revenue guidance raised to $970 million–$1 billion, up from $950 million–$990 million.
- Q2 2026 sales expected at $245 million–$250 million, which would be a new record.
- Anticipates further revenue acceleration in H2 2026 as Army Radio Test Program ramps.
- Focus remains on achieving sustainable high teens adjusted operating margins.


### Segment performance

- Aerospace sales grew 11.7% to $213.8 million; commercial transport up 13.7%, general aviation up 40.7%, military flat.
- IFEC revenue rose 7.4% to $110.7 million (48% of total sales); Lighting and Safety up 1.6% to $52.8 million.
- Flight-Critical Electrical Power sales up 16.2% to $24.8 million; Seat Motion sales nearly tripled to $13.2 million.
- Aerospace operating profit was $35.3 million (16.5% margin), up from $22.3 million (11.6%).
- Aerospace bookings $264.4 million, book-to-bill 1.24, backlog $651.4 million.
- Test systems sales rose 15.4% to $16.8 million; segment returned to slight profitability.
- Test systems bookings $26.1 million, book-to-bill 1.55, backlog $83 million.


### Key financial ratios and metrics

- Adjusted EBITDA margin: 16.4% (up 150 bps year-over-year).
- Adjusted operating margin: 12.8% (up 180 bps).
- Book-to-bill ratio: 1.26 overall; 1.24 in aerospace; 1.55 in test systems.
- Weighted average diluted shares: 38.2 million, down from 43 million due to convertible note repurchase.


### Risk factors and uncertainties

- Ongoing geopolitical conflicts (e.g., Iran War) currently have no impact, but future risks remain uncertain.
- Rising fuel prices could affect low-cost carriers, but these are not major customers.
- Inflationary pressures and memory chip price increases are present but not major drivers.


### Significant events and developments

- $4.6 million revenue contribution from BMA acquisition.
- $2.8 million MV-75 program catch-up adjustment boosted margins.
- $7 million reduction in litigation-related expenses.
- Major operational relocation in Seattle completed smoothly.
- Awaiting Army Radio Test Program production order, expected to contribute $20 million in H2 2026.


### Capital allocation and financing

- Generated $10.6 million cash from operations, down from $20.6 million due to higher working capital needs.
- CapEx was $11.2 million, up from $2.1 million, with full-year 2026 expected at $40–$45 million.
- Total debt at $334.9 million; cash and equivalents $11.9 million; available liquidity $231.8 million.
- Investing $15–$17 million in 2026 for new ERP system, with $2–$3 million as operating expense.


### Market and industry conditions

- Strong demand across all product lines, with no single large order driving bookings.
- Commercial aircraft production rates rising at Airbus and Boeing, supporting growth.
- Airlines increasing investment in IFEC and premium seating to meet passenger expectations.
- eVTOL and drone markets seen as emerging opportunities, but market size and timing remain uncertain.
- Pricing initiatives have largely caught up with inflation, with further improvements expected.
