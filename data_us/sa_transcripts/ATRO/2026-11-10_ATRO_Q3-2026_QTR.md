# Astronics Corporation — Q3 2026 (2026-11-10)

## 요약(stockanalysis 자동 생성)

### Executive summary

- Record Q3 sales of $271.7 million, up 28.5% YoY, with adjusted operating margin of 18.8% and adjusted EBITDA margin of 21.8%.
- Strong demand and record aerospace backlog supported continued growth expectations; both segments improved profitability.


### Financial highlights

- Gross profit rose 46% to $94 million, or 34.6% of sales; gross margin expanded 410 bps, including 70 bps from $1.9 million of tariff refunds.
- Reported operating income doubled to a record $54 million, or 19.9% of sales; adjusted operating income was $51 million, with adjusted margin up 650 bps to 18.8%.
- Net income was $52.2 million, or $1.08 per diluted share, versus a net loss of $11.1 million, or $0.26 per share; adjusted net income was $38.9 million, or $0.81 per share.
- Adjusted EBITDA increased over 81% to $59.3 million; margin was 21.8%, versus 15.5% YoY and 19.8% QoQ.
- YTD sales were $762 million, up 22%; operating margin was 16%, versus 6.6%, and adjusted EBITDA was $149 million, or 19.5% of sales versus 14.3%.


### Outlook and guidance

- FY2026 revenue guidance maintained at $1.02 billion-$1.04 billion, implying 19.5% growth at midpoint; Q4 range is $255 million-$275 million, with midpoint slightly below Q3 record sales.
- Q4 range reflects customer scheduling and possible year-end slippage; backlog and customer feedback support expectations for strong market conditions.
- Early 2027 outlook points to strong double-digit growth, well north of 10%, with continued bottom-line strength; formal revenue guidance expected closer to year-end.
- Incremental margin on incremental sales has been trending around 40%, viewed as realistic for 2027; Q4 R&D is expected to rise on project timing.
- 4549/T program revenue expected at $9 million-$10 million per quarter at full production; Q4 revenue expected to be similar to Q3, subject to supplier capacity.


### Segment performance

- Aerospace sales reached a record $245.5 million, up 27.4%; operating profit was $57.6 million, or 23.4% of sales, while adjusted profit was $55.1 million and adjusted margin 22.5%.
- Aerospace growth spanned major markets: commercial transport +29%, general aviation +52%, military aircraft +5%; IFEC sales rose 24% to $131 million.
- Seat motion sales more than doubled to $24.9 million, including $6.4 million from BMA; lighting and safety rose 17% to $61.6 million, and flight-critical electrical power rose 25% to $20.9 million.
- Aerospace bookings were $258.3 million, book-to-bill 1.05x; backlog reached a record $670 million, up from $657 million in Q2.
- Test Systems sales were $26.2 million, up 40%, including $5.3 million from Army 4549/T; operating profit was $3.9 million, or 14.7% of sales, versus near break-even YoY.


### Key financial ratios and metrics

- Consolidated bookings were $271.1 million, with a 1.00x book-to-bill ratio; ending backlog was $780 million, roughly even with the Q2 record.
- Aerospace adjusted operating margin was 22.5%, up 580 bps YoY and from 21.4% in Q2; consolidated adjusted operating margin reached the high-teens target at 18.8%.
- Test Systems trailing 12-month bookings were $121.8 million, book-to-bill 1.41x; quarterly bookings were $12.8 million, book-to-bill 0.49x, following Q2's $44.7 million Army production award.
- Interest expense declined 37.1% to $1.8 million, reflecting lower debt and reduced rates after the September 2025 refinancing.


### Risk factors and uncertainties

- Global supply-chain constraints for memory and related components have extended lead times and raised prices; customer arrangements have so far limited the impact.
- MV-75 rescheduling and replanning create uncertainty; 2026 revenue is expected near $35 million, with 2027 provisionally held near that level pending Bell direction.
- Airline profitability, oil prices, macroeconomic conditions, the war in Iran, and defense funding or allocation decisions could affect demand or program timing.
- Q4 margin will depend on sales mix and volume; higher receivables and inventory needs increased working capital, including inventory built for anticipated 2027 demand.


### Significant events and developments

- 4549/T Army radio test production ramped, contributing $5.3 million in Q3; management expects the quarterly rate to roughly double by full-rate production in early 2027.
- BMA, acquired in October 2025, contributed $6.4 million to seat motion sales; organic category growth was also described as strong.
- LEO connectivity systems are gaining acceptance for performance advantages over GEO; active participation spans engineering, certification, and hardware, with greater market clarity expected by year-end.
- Legal recoveries included a favorable U.K. patent appeal and a separate settlement; adjusted results exclude the legal settlement benefits.


### Capital allocation and financing

- Cash from operations was $25.8 million in Q3; CapEx was $4.2 million for the quarter and $21.1 million YTD.
- FY2026 CapEx forecast reduced to $25 million-$30 million from $40 million-$45 million due to project timing, not reduced growth investment.
- Long-term debt fell $25.5 million QoQ to $284.8 million and $49.7 million since year-end; quarter-end available liquidity was $278 million.


### Notes to the financial statements

- Q3 gross profit included $1.9 million of tariff refunds; reported operating income also benefited from approximately $4.4 million in U.K. patent appeal recoveries and a separate settlement of approximately $1 million.
- Adjusted net income of $38.9 million excludes legal settlement benefits; prior-year reported net loss included a $32.6 million loss on settlement of debt tied to partial repurchase of 2030 convertible notes.
- Q3 tax benefit was approximately $200,000, including partial valuation allowance reversal and R&D credits; a further $35 million-$40 million deferred tax asset valuation allowance release may be recognized, subject to evidence of future taxable income.
- A $2.2 million revision to estimated costs to complete certain long-term mass transit contracts reduced Q3 Test Systems revenue.


### Market and industry conditions

- Demand drivers include rising aircraft build rates, adoption of high-end IFEC, advanced electrical power systems, premium seat motion, and the 4549/T test program ramp.
- Record aerospace backlog and sustained connectivity demand support growth; airlines are increasingly offering premium seating, while retrofit demand adds to seat-market activity.
- Boeing and Airbus production ramps continue, though recently announced ramp reductions and supplier capacity concerns remain in focus; shipments were in the high 30s shipsets per month and aligned with demand.
- Missile programs, eVTOL development, and defense follow-on awards represent potential growth opportunities; pricing and capacity exercises suggest missile programs could become meaningful over coming years.
