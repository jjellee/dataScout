# Astronics Corporation — Jefferies Global Industrials Conference 2026 (2026-09-09)

## 요약(stockanalysis 자동 생성)

### Business profile and demand

- Aerospace represents ~90% of revenue, Test ~10%; commercial transport contributes ~70% of volume, government/defense ~20%, and business jets ~10% on a trailing 12-month basis.
- Revenue fell to ~$445 million during the pandemic; FY26 revenue is expected to exceed $1 billion for the first time.
- Margin improvement reflects higher volume, pricing, efficiency gains, and simplification; these initiatives remain ongoing.


### Products and growth programs

- In-seat power has ~90% market share; 90% of wide-body and ~65% of narrow-body aircraft have some form of in-seat power.
- Seat Motion revenue is expected to double in 2026; backlog supports continued growth into 2027 and 2028.
- The MV-75 FLRAA program includes the full electrical distribution system; development is underway, with an estimated ~$1 million per aircraft for modeling and a ~$120 million funded development effort.
- Flight-critical power technologies target smaller aircraft, drones, and eVTOL; permanent-magnet and induction machines can last up to 30,000 hours, versus <1,000 hours for traditional machines.
- A $44 million Army radio-test production award begins an anticipated four- to five-year run; the broader IDIQ is ~$215 million, with $30 million-$40 million of incremental annual revenue expected for modeling.


### Second-quarter performance

- Record Q2 sales of $260 million rose 27% YoY; IFEC sales increased 19%, Seat Motion exceeded $22 million and grew 59% organically, and flight-critical power grew 49%.
- Record bookings of $306 million and a 1.18x book-to-bill lifted backlog to a third consecutive record of $781 million; key orders included $27 million for MV-75 development and the $44 million Army radio award.
- Gross profit was $86.9 million and gross margin 33.4%, up 760 bps YoY; improvement included higher volume, productivity, and a $2 million tariff refund, while the prior-year comparison included charges.
- Record operating income was $40.5 million, or 15.6% of sales; adjusted operating income was $43.2 million, or 16.6%. Aerospace adjusted operating margin was 21.4%; Test returned to operating profitability at ~$0.6 million.
- H1 adjusted net income more than doubled to $32.6 million, or $0.70 per diluted share; adjusted EBITDA more than doubled to $51.5 million, with a 19.8% margin versus 12.4% YoY.


### Outlook and capital allocation

- FY26 revenue outlook was raised to $1.02 billion-$1.04 billion; Q3 revenue guidance is $265 million-$275 million, with Q4 expected to be modestly higher.
- FY26 CapEx is expected at $40 million-$45 million; free cash flow is expected to remain positive for the rest of the year.
- Q2 operating cash flow was $30.1 million and free cash flow $24.4 million; trailing 12-month free cash flow reached $60.7 million versus $36 million a year earlier.
- Debt declined ~$24 million from year-end to $310 million; quarter-end liquidity was $253 million, including $238 million available on the revolver.
- Capital priorities are growth investment, debt reduction, and selective M&A focused on strategic fit and returns while preserving funding flexibility.


### Market and technology opportunities

- A GEO-to-LEO shift in aircraft connectivity could drive retrofits and higher penetration; connectivity was cited at 90% of wide-body and 30% of narrow-body aircraft, with more specific market details expected by year-end.
- IFEC upgrades are a continuing cycle; wireless access points hold an estimated 60%-70% aircraft market share, and wireless charging applications are in development.
- Supplier consolidation has created opportunities in aircraft lighting; a major A220 interior lighting program could potentially extend to the A320 or A350.
