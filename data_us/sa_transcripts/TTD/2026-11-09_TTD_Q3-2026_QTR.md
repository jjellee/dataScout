# The Trade Desk, Inc. — Q3 2026 (2026-11-09)

## 요약(stockanalysis 자동 생성)

### Executive summary

- Q3 revenue was $736m, down 0.5% YoY and meaningfully above August outlook; results included an approximately 3-point one-time strategic-partner benefit. Excluding it, underlying performance still exceeded expectations.
- Leadership cited execution, product improvements and customer value as priorities for restoring stronger, more durable growth; Q3 was progress but below long-term expectations.


### Financial highlights

- Adjusted EBITDA was $266m, a 36% margin, meaningfully above expectations.
- GAAP operating expenses were $643m, including $44m of restructuring charges; excluding stock-based compensation and restructuring, expenses were $504m.
- Net income was $38m, or $0.08 per diluted share; adjusted net income was $155m, or $0.33 per diluted share.
- Operating cash flow was $258m and free cash flow was $153m in Q3.


### Outlook and guidance

- Q4 revenue expected to be at least $755m; adjusted EBITDA expected to be at least $280m.
- Q4 outlook assumes some Q3-to-Q4 growth, but not the historical seasonal uplift; guidance reflects a challenging market and does not assume recent positive indicators immediately convert to revenue.
- Political spend was a mid-single-digit share of Q3 business and expected to be slightly higher in Q4; election-year Q4-to-Q1 political spend has historically declined ~25% sequentially.
- 2027 expense growth expected to moderate meaningfully vs. 2026; workforce actions forecast to generate more than $100m gross savings, with some savings reinvested.
- Open measurement is planned to launch in Q4 and is expected to support growth in 2027 and beyond.


### Segment performance

- Video, including CTV, represented a low-50s% share of Q3 business; mobile was high-20s%, display low-double-digits, and audio ~7%.
- Audio had the strongest YoY growth of any channel; medical health, personal finance, travel and political were the strongest-growing verticals.
- APAC revenue grew ~30% YoY in 9M, led by China at 45% YoY growth in Q3; EMEA grew more than 20% YoY in 9M.
- CPG and auto faced pressure, with higher inflation and interest rates affecting auto financing; execution was also identified as a factor to address.
- Client count for the growth team increased more than 200% YoY through 9M, including 23 new clients in Q3.


### Key financial ratios and metrics

- Revenue declined 0.5% YoY; adjusted EBITDA margin was 36%.
- Platform operations expense rose 23% YoY, reflecting infrastructure and AI investment, data-center mix transition costs, and cost classification changes.
- Median active time for core workflows fell 9% for campaign creation, 15% for ad-group creation and 9% for creative uploads after Zuma upgrades.
- In A/B tests across 62 campaigns, the upgraded Koa CPA model improved CPA by a median 21% vs. the prior model, without additional setup changes.
- Audience Unlimited campaigns improved CPA by more than 30% and reduced CPMs by approximately 14%.


### Risk factors and uncertainties

- Political advertising is cyclical; Q1 is consistently seasonally lower than Q4, with election-year sequential declines historically ~25% on an absolute basis.
- Large global advertisers remain under external pressure; Q4 growth is expected below historical seasonal patterns amid a challenging market.
- Higher inflation and interest rates pressured CPG and auto demand; platform operations costs remain an investment area as efficiency and operating leverage are pursued.
- Forward-looking expectations are subject to risks and uncertainties, and actual results may differ materially from projections.


### Significant events and developments

- Co-founder Dave Pickles agreed to substantially expand his role, supporting the next phase of platform and product innovation.
- Workforce was reduced by approximately 15% in Q3 as part of efforts to create a more focused, efficient operating model.
- Joint business plans rose more than 30% YoY through Q3, including more than 20% growth in Q3; participating customers grew meaningfully faster than those under traditional MSAs.
- More than 100 JBPs were in the pipeline; one financial-services client added millions of dollars in spend from a single business line after expanded engagement.
- Zuma launched in Q3; more than 100 advertisers tested Audience Unlimited, with several hundred campaigns live.


### Capital allocation and financing

- Quarter-end cash equivalents and short-term investments totaled about $1.6bn; no Class A share repurchases occurred in Q3.
- Repurchase authorization was expanded, bringing total available authorization to $1bn; capital allocation remains focused on long-term shareholder returns and offsetting dilution over time.
- Option repricing issued no new shares and created no additional dilution, though it increased stock-based compensation modestly.


### Market and industry conditions

- The global advertising market was characterized as roughly $1tn; digital advertising and CTV continue gaining share, while premium content becomes more digitally addressable.
- Advertising supply is expanding faster than demand, increasing advertiser choice and the importance of objective, data- and AI-driven decisioning.
- Customer-owned proprietary data is increasingly valuable in an AI-driven market, elevating demand for trusted, independent partners that protect data.
- Management expects LLM competition and agentic AI to unlock advertising TAM and improve campaign selection, audience creation and optimization.
