# SolarEdge Technologies, Inc. — Q3 2026 (2026-11-04)

## 요약(stockanalysis 자동 생성)

### Executive summary

- Q3 revenue and gross margin met guidance midpoint; Nexis revenue exceeded $100 million, storage was the largest product category for a second consecutive quarter, and free cash flow remained positive for the fifth consecutive quarter.
- Q4 revenue outlook fell below Investor Day expectations amid weak U.S. and German markets, deliberate channel inventory management, and exits from select lower-profit markets.
- Turnaround priorities remain profitable growth, share gains, scaling Nexis, and advancing solid-state transformer (SST) opportunities for data centers.


### Financial highlights

- Q3 GAAP revenue was $326.1 million, down 5.8% QoQ and 4.1% YoY; non-GAAP revenue was $325.4 million, down 5.8% QoQ and 4.2% YoY.
- GAAP gross margin was 33.2%, versus 27.5% in Q2 and 21.2% in Q3 2025; non-GAAP margin was 34.7%, versus 28.6% and 18.8%, respectively.
- Non-GAAP gross margin included a ~$36 million IEEPA tariff benefit; excluding it, estimated margin was ~23.6%, near the midpoint of the 22%-26% guidance range.
- GAAP operating loss was $1.9 million; non-GAAP operating income was $23.9 million, versus $10.2 million in Q2 and a $23.8 million loss in Q3 2025.
- GAAP net loss was $8.4 million and non-GAAP net income was $21.1 million; non-GAAP diluted EPS was $0.29 and adjusted EBITDA was $31.8 million.


### Outlook and guidance

- Q4 2026 revenue guidance is $245 million-$275 million; the $50 million midpoint reduction versus Investor Day comprises $35 million channel inventory management, $10 million market exits, and $5 million FX impact.
- Q4 non-GAAP gross margin guidance is 17%-21%, reflecting fixed-cost under-absorption and excluding any potential IEEPA refund; non-GAAP OpEx guidance is $87 million-$91 million.
- Q1 2027 revenue is expected to decline 10%+ sequentially from winter seasonality and continued U.S. and German regulatory uncertainty.
- Beyond Q1, management expects flat YoY revenue for 2027 and adjusted EBITDA break-even following restructuring; SST customer pilots are expected to conclude during 2027.
- SST milestones remain a working lab system by end-2026, pilot installations in 2027, and volume shipments in 2028.


### Segment performance

- U.S. revenue was $145.9 million, down 5.8% QoQ and 44.7% of total; Europe was $141.7 million, down 8.2% and 43.5%; international was $38.5 million, up 4.3% and 11.8%.
- U.S. rooftop C&I share remains above 50%, with a healthy pipeline; market demand is supported by electricity prices, data-center demand, and expected storage attach-rate growth.
- Nexis systems generated more than $100 million in Q3 revenue; European three-phase adoption is growing, with higher system sizes and storage attachment, while U.S. rollout began in July.
- Battery upsell revenue from the installed base in the Netherlands and Germany exceeded $30 million, up from more than $20 million in Q2; storage was the largest product category for a second straight quarter.
- SST development advanced: 800 VDC conversion operated under load in the lab, the SST cell achieved >99% efficiency, and high-voltage tests supported the 34.5 kV design target.


### Key financial ratios and metrics

- Non-GAAP OpEx was $88.8 million, versus $88.5 million in Q2 and $87.7 million in Q3 2025; GAAP OpEx was $110.1 million.
- Operating cash flow was $20.4 million and free cash flow was $7.8 million in Q3; YTD free cash flow was $31.7 million, marking five consecutive positive quarters.
- Cash equivalents, restricted cash, and marketable securities net of debt totaled $260.9 million at September 30; cash and equivalents were $555.1 million.
- Trade receivables were $209.6 million and DSO rose to 60 days from 57; inventory increased to $621.1 million from $599.8 million QoQ.


### Risk factors and uncertainties

- U.S. residential demand remains weak amid higher interest rates and delays in FEOC/PFE guidance; management expects uncertainty to persist into Q1 2027.
- German renewable-policy reform could reduce feed-in-tariff support and weigh on installations; regulatory uncertainty is driving cautious channel planning.
- Q4 and Q1 seasonality, lower volumes, fixed-cost under-absorption, and euro exchange rates pressure near-term revenue and margins.
- California’s potential solar and storage property-tax change may have some effect, though management does not expect a major impact.


### Significant events and developments

- Safe Harbor transactions total $1.7 billion and could reach $3.3 billion with required optimizers, supporting demand visibility in residential and C&I markets.
- Nexis is approved by leading U.S. TPO and prepaid-lease providers; California’s top utility approved the meter-collar solution, enabling rollout.
- SST work included 800 VDC protection and grounding guidance developed with NVIDIA and extended collaboration with Infineon on solid-state circuit protection.
- Hyperscalers and neoclouds are discussing SST testing; management expects 800 VDC adoption as data-center power architectures evolve.


### Capital allocation and financing

- Restructuring and resource reallocation are expected to generate mid-single-digit-million-dollar net savings each quarter, including ~$3 million in COGS and $2 million in OpEx; full expense benefit begins Q1 2027.
- De-emphasized markets represent ~$40 million-$50 million in annual revenue; associated revenue headwinds begin in Q4, while resources shift toward core markets and SST investment.
- Q3 capex was $12.6 million; FY 2026 capex guidance is ~$45 million-$55 million, funding SST, manufacturing capacity, and a new Israel headquarters with expanded R&D facilities.
- Full-year 2026 positive free cash flow remains expected, supported by operating improvements, expense and capex discipline, and monetization of 45X credits.


### Notes to the financial statements

- Q3 results included an approximately $36 million IEEPA tariff-related benefit; approximately $4 million was expected to be returned in Q4, with Q4 margin guidance excluding potential refund impact.


### Market and industry conditions

- U.S. residential solar remains financing-sensitive; Germany is awaiting renewable-policy reform, while U.S. C&I demand benefits from rising power prices and data-center needs.
- Management expects storage attachment and retrofit demand to rise; Nexis, TPO relationships, and Safe Harbor visibility are positioned to benefit from the shift to PV-plus-storage.
- Data-center power demand is driving interest in 800 VDC; management sees 34.5 kV as relevant to large facilities and expects pilot activity in 2027.
