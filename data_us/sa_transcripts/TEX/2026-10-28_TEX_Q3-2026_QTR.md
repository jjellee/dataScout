# Terex Corporation — Q3 2026 (2026-10-28)

## 요약(stockanalysis 자동 생성)

### Executive summary

- Q3 sales were $2.2B, up 11.6% YoY pro forma; adjusted EBITDA rose 14% pro forma to $286M, with margin expansion and stronger cash generation.
- Pro forma orders increased 53% YoY to $2.3B; backlog reached $7B and consolidated book-to-bill was ~103%.
- Materials Processing and Specialty Vehicles posted growth and margin expansion; Aerials improved sequentially, while Environmental Solutions faced ramp-up costs and lower sales.


### Financial highlights

- Reported sales were $2.2B, up $810M or 58%; pro forma sales increased $229M or 11.6% YoY.
- Adjusted EBITDA rose $35M to $286M; adjusted EBITDA margin was 13% versus 12.8% pro forma a year earlier; adjusted diluted EPS was $1.50.
- Operating cash flow was $241M and free cash flow was $209M; capital expenditures were $32M.


### Outlook and guidance

- FY26 sales guidance is $8B-$8.2B, with pro forma growth of ~8% at midpoint; adjusted pro forma EBITDA is $930M-$960M, excluding Capacity and LayMor.
- FY26 adjusted EBITDA is expected to grow ~10% YoY, with an 11.7% margin at midpoint; guidance includes ~$28M of realized synergies and excludes cost to achieve synergies, purchase accounting adjustments, and other non-recurring items.
- FY26 EPS is expected at $4.80-$5; free cash flow guidance increased to $360M-$400M and capex is expected at $145M.
- Segment sales outlook: Environmental Solutions approximately flat YoY; Materials Processing low-double-digit growth; Specialty Vehicles high-single-digit growth; Aerials low-double-digit growth, described as mid-teens.
- Q4 includes normal seasonality, freight inflation, and continued Waukesha ramp inefficiencies; those inefficiencies are expected to normalize entering 2027, with no FY27 guidance yet.


### Segment performance

- Environmental Solutions sales fell 8% YoY to $400M; adjusted EBITDA margin declined 390 bps to 15.3%, reflecting mix, Waukesha ramp inefficiencies, and lower ESG volume absorption.
- Materials Processing sales rose 11.3% to $434M; adjusted EBITDA margin expanded 530 bps to 19.3% on higher volume, favorable mix, price-cost discipline, and improved absorption.
- Specialty Vehicles sales increased 8.1% to $657M; adjusted EBITDA margin improved 300 bps to 16%, supported by mix and operating efficiency, partly offset by inflation.
- Aerials sales grew 32% YoY to $709M; adjusted EBITDA margin was 8.7%, down 180 bps YoY but up 300 bps QoQ as volume and cost actions partly offset tariffs, inflation, and mix.
- Segment bookings rose across all four businesses; Materials Processing backlog nearly doubled to $676M, Specialty Vehicles backlog grew despite higher throughput, and Aerials backlog increased $455M YoY to $852M.


### Key financial ratios and metrics

- Adjusted EBITDA margin was 13%, versus 12.8% pro forma in the prior-year quarter; FY26 midpoint margin guidance is 11.7%.
- Net working capital was 15% of sales versus 18.1% pro forma a year earlier, primarily reflecting lower inventory and the REV merger.
- Quarter-end net debt was $2.2B, including $407M cash; net leverage was 2.3x net debt to trailing 12-month adjusted EBITDA on a pro forma basis.
- FY26 interest and other expenses are expected to be ~$180M on average debt outstanding of $2.6B; the effective tax rate is now expected at 19%.


### Risk factors and uncertainties

- Tariffs and inflation remain meaningful industrial-sector pressures; freight costs rose in Q3 and are expected to persist into Q4, with customer price recovery lagging costs by 3-9 months.
- Environmental Solutions faces Waukesha production ramp inefficiencies through Q4; recovery in refuse collection demand is now expected to follow a U-shaped rather than V-shaped pattern.
- Q4 seasonality reduces shipments in Aerials and Specialty Vehicles; Materials Processing faces unfavorable product mix, including more material handling and concrete.
- Inflation is not assumed to ease in 2027; pricing actions and cost mitigation are intended to support price-cost neutrality.


### Significant events and developments

- Aerials carve-out work advanced, with active engagement with multiple strategic counterparties; no transaction timeline or counterparty details were provided in discussions.
- Capacity and LayMor exits simplify the portfolio; the divested businesses represented $125M-$150M of annualized revenue through cycles and mid-single-digit to high-single-digit EBITDA.
- Centralized procurement launched; integration work is shifting toward synergy capture, productivity, quality, and operational improvement.
- New products included Heil's automated side loader, Finlay's hybrid Elektro crushing range, and Genie's next-generation Slab Scissor Lifts, named Product of the Year.


### Capital allocation and financing

- Returned $91M to shareholders in Q3, including $72M in share repurchases and $19M in dividends; also repaid $113M of debt.
- Board approved a new $500M repurchase authorization; combined with $111M remaining from prior authorizations, total capacity was $611M at quarter-end.
- Capital allocation priorities include balance-sheet strength, lower working capital, optimized capex, shareholder returns, and organic investment.


### Market and industry conditions

- Demand drivers remain supportive across infrastructure, utilities, power, and data centers; U.S. municipal replacement demand supports fire and emergency equipment.
- U.S. commercial and residential construction remain below historical levels but largely stabilized; Europe is mixed.
- Refuse collection demand improved sequentially as channel adjustments eased and fleet utilization normalized; engine-regulation pre-buys have largely fallen out of expectations.
- Specialty Vehicles has strong municipal replacement demand, but lead times exceed 24 months versus an estimated sustainable industry level of 12 months; capacity is being added selectively.
