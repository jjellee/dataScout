# ATI Inc. — Q3 2026 (2026-10-29)

## 요약(stockanalysis 자동 생성)

### Executive summary

- Revenue rose 26% YoY to $1.4B and adjusted EBITDA increased 46% to $328M, exceeding the high end of guidance by $13M.
- Full-year adjusted EBITDA guidance increased $35M to $1.19B-$1.20B, the third raise this year; Q4 is expected to be the strongest quarter of 2026 for sales and profit.
- Strong demand across end markets, near-record backlog of $4.3B, durable contract pricing, and a richer Aerospace & Defense mix supported performance and outlook.


### Financial highlights

- Q3 revenue was $1.4B, up 26% YoY and 13% sequentially; A&D accounted for nearly 70% of quarterly revenue.
- Adjusted EBITDA was $328M, up 46% YoY and $13M above the high end of guidance; adjusted EBITDA margin expanded 300 bps to 23%.
- Adjusted EPS increased 72% to $1.46; adjusted free cash flow was $80M in Q3 and $223M YTD, up $73M or 49% YoY.


### Outlook and guidance

- FY adjusted EBITDA guidance rose $35M to $1.19B-$1.20B, implying 39% growth vs. 2025; adjusted EPS guidance is $5.17-$5.23.
- Q4 adjusted EBITDA is forecast at $345M-$355M and adjusted EPS at $1.51-$1.57; midpoint EBITDA implies 51% YoY growth and 7% sequential growth.
- FY consolidated adjusted EBITDA margin is expected in the low-20% range, with ~50% incremental margins; FY adjusted free cash flow guidance remains $550M-$600M, midpoint $575M.
- FY jet engine revenue growth outlook increased to low-to-mid-20%; airframe growth remains mid-to-high single digits, defense high teens, and specialty energy mid-teens.
- Q4 segment margins: AA&S in the mid-20% range and HPMC in the mid-20% range; HPMC ramp-related margin impact is expected to be fully behind it by year-end.


### Segment performance

- AA&S sales increased 34% YoY to $702M; segment margin reached a record 25.8%, up 850 bps, on pricing, volume, and favorable A&D and specialty energy mix.
- A&D reached ~45% of AA&S sales, versus 18% in 2021; the segment's full-year EBITDA margin outlook is low-to-mid-20%, with management describing mid-20% profitability as durable.
- HPMC sales increased 20% YoY to $721M, with a 23.2% segment margin; a new nickel superalloy ramp reduced Q3 margin by ~200 bps versus plan.
- The new alloy moved from qualification to production in nine months; HPMC is one of only two global producers, and the ramp expands next-generation engine content.
- Jet engine revenue rose 39% YoY and 19% sequentially; airframe revenue increased 11% YoY and 14% sequentially, while defense reached record revenue after 6% YoY growth.


### Key financial ratios and metrics

- Adjusted EBITDA margin was 23%, up 300 bps YoY; AA&S margin was 25.8%, up 850 bps, and HPMC margin was 23.2%.
- Managed working capital was 32.9% of sales at quarter-end, improving 140 bps sequentially.
- Backlog was $4.3B, up 20% YoY and near an all-time record; extended lead times support visibility for differentiated products.


### Risk factors and uncertainties

- New-alloy production inefficiencies reduced HPMC Q3 margin by ~200 bps versus plan; impact is expected to diminish in Q4 and be fully behind the segment by year-end.
- Inventory is expected to remain temporarily elevated as new capacity comes online, though inventory velocity improved in Q4 and levels are expected to normalize over time.
- Specialty energy shipments can vary quarter to quarter as flexible assets shift production between defense and energy orders; underlying market demand was described as unchanged.


### Significant events and developments

- A raw-material recovery and reuse project now supplies most ongoing operating needs, improving supply security and expected to generate several million dollars of annual savings from 2027.
- Mexico facility output tripled in Q3; deferred revenue of $30M-$40M is being shipped across Q3 and Q4, with the facility expected to exit 2026 at or above its original footprint run rate.
- Richland furnace qualifications for most standard-quality products are expected by year-end, with production in 2027; more demanding engine-alloy qualifications extend into mid-2027.


### Capital allocation and financing

- Q3 capital expenditures were $93M; YTD CapEx totaled $217M, with $48M of customer-funded capital, and growth projects remained on schedule and budget.
- FY gross CapEx guidance remains $280M-$300M, partially offset by $55M-$65M of customer-funded CapEx.
- Share repurchases remain a priority for incremental free cash flow; $70M had been repurchased by Q3-end, with $425M remaining under authorization.
- Organic investment will prioritize differentiated capabilities and customer alignment through contracts or customer-funded capital; no monolithic assets are planned.


### Market and industry conditions

- Demand remained strong across jet engines, airframe, defense, and specialty energy; next-generation engines use more than twice the content of legacy engines.
- Jet engine demand spans new production and MRO; management reported no demand impact from higher fuel prices and cited customer schedules aligned with production.
- Airframe inventories were described as largely normalized, with orders aligned to production schedules and demand expected to build through year-end and into 2027.
- Commercial nuclear and industrial gas turbine demand remained healthy; defense demand strengthened, including inquiries for titanium, nickel, and niobium products for strategic programs.
