# Wayfair Inc. — Q3 2026 (2026-11-04)

## 요약(stockanalysis 자동 생성)

### Executive summary

- Q3 revenue grew 9.7% YoY, the strongest growth quarter in the post-pandemic period; U.S. revenue rose 11.4% as orders and customer growth supported share gains.
- Adjusted EBITDA was $236 million, with a 6.9% margin, the strongest Q3 margin since 2020.
- Management expects share gains and Adjusted EBITDA growth to continue as customer loyalty, product-led growth, stores, and new discovery channels mature.


### Financial highlights

- Net revenue rose 9.7% YoY; orders increased 12.2%, while AOV declined ~2%, largely from mix shift as higher-AOV business lines narrowed their spread with wayfair.com.
- Gross margin was 29.6% of revenue; customer service and merchant fees were 3.7%, advertising was 9.9%, and Contribution Margin was 16.1%.
- Adjusted EBITDA was $236 million, or 6.9% of revenue; net income was $62 million, improving by more than $160 million YoY.
- Operating cash flow was $198 million; capex of $62 million resulted in $136 million of Free Cash Flow, up more than 40% YoY.


### Outlook and guidance

- Q4 revenue guidance is high-single-digit growth; momentum was positive into the quarter following Way Day.
- Q4 gross margin guidance is 29.5%-30.5%, targeting the lower end; customer service and merchant fees are expected near Q3 levels.
- Q4 advertising is guided to 10.5%-11.5% of revenue, reflecting holiday-season investment; management expects YoY advertising leverage versus Q4 2025.
- Q4 SOTG&A is guided to $360 million-$370 million and Adjusted EBITDA margin to 6%-7%; Contribution Margin is expected near 15%.
- Q4 outlook includes equity compensation and related taxes of $60 million-$70 million, D&A of $59 million-$65 million, net interest expense of $42 million, 138 million weighted-average shares, and capex of $70 million-$80 million.


### Segment performance

- U.S. revenue grew 11.4% YoY against a roughly flat category, with management indicating the U.S. share spread reached double digits.
- International revenue declined 2.3% YoY; Canada and U.K. home furnishings markets remained weaker than the U.S., and international Adjusted EBITDA loss widened to $25 million.
- International initiatives are at an earlier stage: Rewards launched later than in the U.S., while Delivery+ and stores have yet to roll out in other markets.
- Chicago, Atlanta, and Columbus stores showed similar positive market-level sales lift; five additional stores are planned to open next year.


### Key financial ratios and metrics

- Active customers increased more than 4% YoY; new customer orders grew over 14%, and total orders rose 12.2%.
- Advertising was 9.9% of revenue, below 10% for the first time since 2021; revenue from direct visits rose more than 15% YoY.
- Conversion rate improved by more than 30 bps since early 2025; branded-search visits increased over 17% in Q3.
- AI-referred visits more than doubled sequentially and reached roughly 1% of total traffic.


### Risk factors and uncertainties

- Q3 SOTG&A of $378 million exceeded guidance, including one-time costs such as the U.K. Competition and Markets Authority settlement.
- Q3 advertising cost outperformance partly reflected timing; holiday-season spending is expected to lift the advertising rate in Q4.
- International performance remains exposed to weaker category conditions and delayed rollout of growth initiatives; the U.K. settlement also weighed on international EBITDA.


### Significant events and developments

- Creator-content sales rose more than 70% during the latest Way Day versus the prior fall event; program sales had already exceeded full-year 2025, with storefronts planned for 2027.
- AI-referred traffic is growing; partnerships include Google’s Universal Commerce Protocol, a Wayfair-branded shopping agent on OpenAI, and an AI-optimized catalog live in Meta Muse.
- Revenue from text messages rose over 30% as the text audience grew more than 40% over the past year.
- Wayfair Rewards membership and member engagement were on track, with management describing the program as early in its growth and behavior-change trajectory.
- Atlanta and Columbus store performance was similar to Chicago; five stores are scheduled to open next year.


### Capital allocation and financing

- Cash and equivalents were $1.1 billion; total liquidity, including the revolving credit facility, was $1.5 billion.
- Repurchased $60 million of shares, fully offsetting Q3 stock-based compensation issuance and keeping the share count roughly flat QoQ; management intends to make the offset recurring.
- S&P and Moody’s upgraded credit ratings, following Fitch’s upgrade earlier in the spring.
- Capex is expected to rise with store investment, which management expects to self-fund while supporting growing Free Cash Flow.


### Market and industry conditions

- The U.S. home furnishings category remained roughly flat in Q3; management described the broader category as near the low end of its cycle, with no clear recovery signs.
- Canada and the U.K. category conditions continued to lag the U.S. market.
- Management attributed share gains primarily to structural improvements in assortment, price, speed, delivery, marketing, and customer confidence rather than macro conditions.
