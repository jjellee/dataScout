# Super Group (SGHC) Limited — Q3 2026 (2026-11-04)

## 요약(stockanalysis 자동 생성)

### Executive summary

- Q3 revenue rose 10% YoY to $611M and Adjusted EBITDA increased 8% to $165M; favorable sports outcomes for customers softened the quarter, while iCasino growth partly offset the impact.
- Average monthly active customers increased 9% YoY to 6M; activity rebounded with the return of major football leagues, reaching 6.2M in September.
- Management cited strong Q4 momentum, growing casino activity and October sports margin at its highest level of 2026 to date.


### Financial highlights

- Q3 revenue was $611M, up 10% YoY; Adjusted EBITDA was $165M, up 8%, with a 27% margin.
- Customer-friendly sports results reduced sports margin to 12.1% versus a trailing 24-month average of 13.7%, impacting revenue by ~$40M and Adjusted EBITDA by ~$25M.
- Total wagering rose 11% for casino and 4% for sports; record iCasino wagers grew 11% YoY and 14% excluding the U.S.
- YTD free cash flow conversion was 70%; cash ended the quarter at $602M, up 30% YoY.


### Outlook and guidance

- FY2026 revenue guidance revised to $2.525B–$2.575B and Adjusted EBITDA to $700M–$720M, assuming Q4 sports margin is within its normal range.
- Management said the revision reflects Q3 sports results, not iGaming weakness; Q4 began strongly, with October sports margin the highest of the year.
- For 2027, guidance is expected to assume organic growth, sports margin within the expected range, disciplined marketing and no aggressive customer-persistency assumptions; launched Alberta operations would be included.
- Namibia launch is expected in 2027, contingent on local policy adoption; New Zealand’s regulated launch is expected around mid-2027, with Kenya also targeted for a possible 2027 return subject to relaunch requirements.


### Segment performance

- Africa revenue grew 17% YoY to $262M; Adjusted EBITDA increased 23% to $88M, a 34% margin. Sports wagers rose 6% and casino wagers 21%.
- International revenue increased 5% YoY, or 9% excluding the U.S., to $344M; Adjusted EBITDA grew 24% to $93M, with margin expansion of more than 400 bps despite higher U.K. taxes.
- Europe revenue rose 6%, led by U.K. growth of 13%; North America revenue excluding the U.S. increased 10%, while Canada excluding Ontario and Alberta grew 15%.
- Rest-of-world revenue increased 5%; New Zealand grew 3% during a marketing hiatus, and three of 15 available casino licenses were secured.


### Key financial ratios and metrics

- Q3 Adjusted EBITDA margin was 27%; Africa Adjusted EBITDA margin was 34%.
- Sports margin was 12.1% in Q3 versus 13.7% for the trailing 24 months; YTD sports margin was 13.8%, within the expected structural range.
- YTD free cash flow conversion was 70%; cash was $602M at quarter-end.
- Marketing spend is expected to remain within 21%–22% of revenue in 2027; management described current marketing levels as roughly 20%–22%.


### Risk factors and uncertainties

- Sports outcomes are volatile and outside management’s control; Q3 customer-friendly results reduced revenue by ~$40M and Adjusted EBITDA by ~$25M.
- A proposed South African national tax could add 20% of GGR on top of existing provincial tax and VAT; it remains a discussion proposal requiring further consultation and legislative approvals, with updates expected in the February 2027 budget.
- Market entry and launches depend on regulatory, tax and licensing conditions, including Namibia policy adoption, Rwanda’s tax framework and Kenya’s license reapplication.
- Alberta customers are adapting to new regulated-market product rules, including removal of autoplay; customer migration was completed in October.


### Significant events and developments

- Betway’s Manchester United partnership launched with the new football season, generating positive customer engagement, particularly in Africa.
- Nigeria, Tanzania and Zambia casino licenses were secured, along with sports betting and casino licenses in Rwanda; Mozambique’s casino tax changes improve its commercial outlook.
- Three New Zealand licenses were secured; online slots in Germany are expected to go live after completing separate-wallet technology work, although deposit limits remain a constraint.
- Supercoin is live on South Africa’s three largest exchanges; wallet functionality is expected to expand this quarter, with a full public launch planned for early 2027.


### Capital allocation and financing

- The board authorized a $150M share repurchase program, to be executed with discipline and without a committed pace or price.
- Capital priorities are organic investment first, selective external growth at the right price second, and shareholder returns from excess cash, including dividends and buybacks.
- Approximately $223M was returned to shareholders over the last 12 months, including around $25M of dividends in Q3; the regular dividend remains the foundation of returns.
- Management intends to grow the regular dividend over time while preserving balance-sheet flexibility for investment and acquisition opportunities.


### Market and industry conditions

- Summer seasonality across European football moderated customer activity; engagement rebounded as major leagues resumed, reaching 6.2M active customers in September.
- African parlay wagering can produce larger sports-result swings and a longer customer-winnings recycling cycle than international books; management cited stronger retention and margins in the African model.
- New Zealand’s marketing hiatus is expected to end after regulated licensing, while Alberta’s regulated market transition is expected to create a more rational competitive environment than Ontario.
- Management cited leaner costs, disciplined marketing and operating efficiencies as support for growth and margin expansion despite higher gaming taxes.
