# MercadoLibre, Inc. — Q3 2026 (2026-11-04)

## 요약(stockanalysis 자동 생성)

### Executive summary

- Net revenue and financial income grew 41% YoY on an FX-neutral basis, with broad-based growth across businesses and countries; Brazil led, including 36% YoY GMV growth.
- Engagement and ecosystem usage strengthened, while deliberate shipping and marketing investments reduced quarterly operating margin; management said investments are intended to build long-term earnings power.


### Financial highlights

- Income from operations was $676 million; operating margin was 6.3%, down 40 bps QoQ.
- Items per buyer grew 16% YoY in Q3, after fully lapping the prior-year free-shipping threshold change.
- Sales and marketing rose 40 bps sequentially to 11.6% of net revenue, driven in part by increased Brazil affiliate investment.


### Outlook and guidance

- Part of the Q3 logistics-capacity cost headwind is expected to ease in Q4 as new capacity fills; energy-cost pressure is expected to remain.
- Capacity will continue to expand to support growth, though not necessarily at Q3's pace; each facility improves unit economics as it fills.
- Near-term investment areas are expected to remain broadly similar; management cited no significant new investment areas or sharp change in quick-commerce spending.


### Segment performance

- Brazil commerce GMV grew 36% YoY; orders through the affiliate channel grew 240% YoY, and the affiliate network exceeded 12 million members regionally.
- Fintech monthly active users rose 29%, assets under management 67%, and the credit portfolio 62%; advertising grew 58% YoY.
- Acquiring grew 42% YoY with healthy profit margins; take rates declined with movement upmarket.
- Credit cards saw record issuance; faster growth in cards relative to the mature book increases near-term costs, while merchant and consumer loan books remained healthy.


### Key financial ratios and metrics

- Operating margin was 6.3%, down 40 bps QoQ; shipping was a roughly 60 bps sequential headwind, split between 40 bps in COGS and 20 bps in OpEx.
- Brazil cost per shipment declined 5% YoY and 14% since before the free-shipping threshold reduction, on an FX-neutral basis.
- 15–90-day NPLs were 7%, stable QoQ and YoY; Argentina NPLs improved across products to near historical lows.


### Risk factors and uncertainties

- Credit conditions weakened in Brazil and Argentina; lending growth continued with greater caution in riskier segments, and risk management remained the priority.
- Higher energy and fuel prices continued to pressure shipping costs; some costs were passed through to consumers, but not all.
- Argentina's margin trajectory remains uncertain amid cross-border competition; management cited stable recent competitive conditions and declined to give margin outlook.


### Significant events and developments

- AI investment increased $30 million QoQ; search conversion and click-through gains were said to more than offset model costs, with efficiency gains also reported in customer support and software development.
- An in-house shopping agent became available to a small share of users in the three largest markets; results were described as early but promising, and inventory was not being offered to external platforms.
- Brazil operations shifted logistics schedules to five days on and two days off in August; no material cost impact is expected from that schedule change, while future workweek reductions remain under assessment.


### Capital allocation and financing

- Management is reinvesting profits from faster-growing businesses into ecosystem initiatives, prioritizing long-term returns over a specific short-term margin target.
- Affiliate investment increased 40 bps QoQ; the channel is a strategic initiative, with spending calibrated to performance and potential for improved unit economics as sellers and brands co-fund campaigns.
- Fulfillment square footage expanded nearly 20% QoQ ahead of peak season, adding capacity for both seasonal demand and future growth.


### Market and industry conditions

- Social media is becoming an additional product-discovery channel; management expects social commerce to gain relevance, while organic traffic and the broader value proposition remain central to e-commerce.
- Management said Brazil's election outcome does not change strategic priorities, citing long-term commerce and fintech opportunities across political cycles.
- A Brazilian constitutional amendment moves the standard schedule to five days on and two off and reduces the legal workweek from 44 to 40 hours; further implementation effects are still being assessed.
