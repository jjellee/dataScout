# AST SpaceMobile, Inc. — Q3 2026 (2026-11-09)

## 요약(stockanalysis 자동 생성)

### Executive summary

- Four priorities advanced: service readiness, satellite production, government programs, and launch access; intermittent nationwide service targeted before year-end 2026.
- Q3 revenue was $42.3 million; cash, cash equivalents, and restricted cash totaled over $3.2 billion at September 30, 2026.
- Launch agreements cover more than 100 BlueBird satellites for deployment in 2027–2028; Blacklift launches are planned to begin in late 2028.


### Financial highlights

- Q3 revenue of $42.3 million came primarily from commercial gateway deliveries and U.S. government service milestones; revenue increased sequentially and YoY.
- Non-GAAP adjusted operating expenses were $133.6 million, versus $119.1 million in Q2; excluding adjusted cost of revenues, expenses were $109.4 million.
- Q3 capital expenditures were ~$310 million, down from ~$610 million in Q2 and below the $350–$425 million outlook, mainly due to shifted launch-payment timing.
- Q3 adjusted operating expenses, excluding adjusted cost of revenues, included a favorable one-time $4.5 million R&D tax credit.


### Outlook and guidance

- FY2026 revenue guidance reiterated at $150–$200 million, with potential upside depending on Q4 milestones and J-LEO timing; revenue expected to build sequentially through 2026.
- Q4 adjusted operating expenses, excluding adjusted cost of revenues, estimated at $130–$140 million; Q4 capex estimated at $400–$500 million, primarily reflecting launch-payment timing.
- Intermittent nationwide service in key markets is targeted by year-end 2026; consumer beta trials are expected in early 2027, with ~25 satellites considered preferable but some flexibility.
- Management sees revenue approaching $1 billion in the 12 months following commercial service launch; no calendar-2027 revenue guidance was issued.
- Several hundred million dollars of incremental Blacklift capex is expected in 2027; average capital cost for a constellation of over 90 BlueBirds estimated at $21–$23 million per satellite, excluding certain initial satellites and launches.


### Segment performance

- Q3 revenue was driven mainly by gateway deliveries and U.S. government milestones; expected revenue sources include gateway sales, government contracts, MNO consulting, and software licensing.
- Continuous service across key markets is expected with 45–60 BlueBirds; ~90 satellites are expected to support additional strategic worldwide markets.
- U.S. Space Force awarded an approximately $100 million advanced-sensing contract; the award supports space-based sensing and is described as an initial step toward a larger AMTI program.


### Key financial ratios and metrics

- Non-GAAP adjusted operating expenses increased $14.5 million QoQ, primarily from higher engineering service costs, adjusted cost of revenues, G&A, and R&D.
- Q3 adjusted operating expenses excluding adjusted cost of revenues were within the previously guided $105–$115 million range.
- Cash, cash equivalents, and restricted cash were over $3.2 billion at September 30, including $1.15 billion gross proceeds from July’s convertible-note offering.
- Convertible notes total $1.15 billion, carry a 1.625% coupon, mature in 2034, and include a capped-call hedge raising the effective conversion price to $149.20 per share.


### Risk factors and uncertainties

- FY2026 revenue depends on successful satellite launches and deployment, government milestone completion, gateway sales, and commercial service activation.
- Quarterly revenue and capex vary with contract timing, equipment deliveries, government milestones, and launch-payment schedules; management cautioned that planned expense and capex changes may be delayed or unrealized.
- Per-satellite cost estimates may fluctuate with geopolitical factors and could rise as launch schedules are accelerated.
- Blacklift requires substantial incremental investment and development; initial launches are planned for late 2028.


### Significant events and developments

- AT&T and the team validated the first satellite-beam handover between ground and space cellular broadband networks.
- BlueBird 11, 12, and 13 are operating in orbit; BlueBird 14–16 shipped to Cape Canaveral awaiting SpaceX launch-date confirmation.
- ASIC integration moved forward to BlueBird 34; the 7-nanometer ASIC is expected to provide 10x processing bandwidth, increasing capacity per satellite and lowering cost per delivered gigabyte.
- Production target is six ASIC satellites per month by December 2026; ~45 satellites are targeted in orbit by mid-2027.
- Blacklift is a planned 20-ton reusable rocket, designed to carry up to five BlueBirds per launch, with launches targeted for late 2028.


### Capital allocation and financing

- July financing included $1.15 billion of 1.625% convertible senior notes due 2034; proceeds strengthened funding for constellation deployment and Blacklift development.
- Japan’s J-LEO initiative provides ~$1 billion in non-dilutive funding for mid-band satellite manufacturing; the joint venture will hold 49% equity, with the majority of proceeds expected in 2027.
- J-LEO funding is expected to subsidize satellites and launch; remaining capacity outside Japan is intended for the global mid-band network, with a low-single-digit net revenue share to the joint venture.
- A further ~$1.2 billion of quasi-governmental non-dilutive financing is in active pipeline evaluation, including subsidy programs and potential first-lien or capex-support debt; timing for a larger component extends into mid-2027.
- Blacklift transaction payments combine cash, stock, and deferred amounts; several hundred million dollars of incremental 2027 capex is expected for equipment, facilities, development, testing, and launch infrastructure.


### Market and industry conditions

- More than 60 MNO partners collectively represent over 3 billion subscribers; initial service markets include the U.S., Canada, Japan, Saudi Arabia, and the U.K.
- Management cited growing demand for direct-to-device connectivity and adjacent markets including sensing, radar, secure communications, sovereign networks, IoT, and AI inference.
- Sovereign satellite-network interest is growing; several international discussions are active, while the U.S. is described as the largest sovereign constellation opportunity.
- Spectrum strategy combines MNO spectrum with controlled MSS spectrum; targets include ~100 MHz of U.S. access and over 60 MHz globally.
