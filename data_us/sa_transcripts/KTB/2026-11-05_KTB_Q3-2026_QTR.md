# Kontoor Brands, Inc. — Q3 2026 (2026-11-05)

## 요약(stockanalysis 자동 생성)

### Executive summary

- Q3 results were in line with expectations; continuing-operations revenue grew 3%, with Wrangler and Helly Hansen each up 3%.
- Lee sale closed Nov. 2 for $698M net proceeds, sharpening focus on Wrangler and Helly Hansen and supporting a $450M Q4 share-repurchase plan.
- Project Jeanius and the Lee transaction are complete; management expects the portfolio transition and focused investment to support stronger growth and returns.


### Financial highlights

- Continuing-operations revenue was $685M, up 3%; adjusted gross margin rose 230 bps to 48.1%.
- Adjusted operating income increased 6% to $101M; adjusted operating margin rose 40 bps to 14.7%.
- Adjusted EPS from continuing operations rose 40% to $1.50; reported EPS was a loss of $0.62, including a $1.73 non-cash IP-migration charge.
- Inventory was $570M, down 6% YoY; cash from operations outlook fell to $325M, reflecting inventory investment, Lee-related cash impacts and separation costs.


### Outlook and guidance

- FY revenue is expected at $2.68B, up ~12% including the 53rd week in 2025; Q4 revenue is expected to rise ~2% excluding that week.
- FY adjusted gross margin is expected at 50.0%-50.2%, up 350-370 bps; adjusted operating income is ~ $400M, up ~12%, and adjusted EPS is $5.40, up 31%.
- Q4 Wrangler revenue is expected to grow ~1%; Helly Hansen revenue is expected to grow ~5% excluding the 53rd week.
- 2027 adjusted EPS is expected to be at least $6.60, with cash flow from continuing operations of at least $325M; cash flow is expected to exceed $400M beyond 2027.
- Helly Hansen’s 2030 commitments include double-digit revenue CAGR to more than $1.1B, doubling operating margin and more than $500M cumulative cash flow.


### Segment performance

- Wrangler revenue was $485M, up 3%; U.S. revenue rose 2% and international revenue 7%, led by DTC, female, international and distribution gains.
- Wrangler denim and non-denim bottoms grew at a mid-single-digit rate; men’s-bottoms POS growth slowed from 3% through July to 1% in September.
- Helly Hansen revenue was $191M, up 3%; sport revenue was $147M, up 2%, and workwear was $44M, up 4%.
- Helly Hansen YTD revenue grew 8% on a reported pro forma basis, ahead of the acquisition plan; China JV revenue rose ~65% with strong profitability and is excluded from consolidated revenue.
- Wrangler’s YTD Western business grew high single digits, DTC low double digits and female at a high-teen rate; retail partner inventory declined at a high-single-digit rate.


### Key financial ratios and metrics

- Adjusted gross margin was 48.1%, up 230 bps YoY; adjusted operating margin was 14.7%, up 40 bps.
- Adjusted EPS was $1.50, up 40%; reported continuing-operations EPS was -$0.62.
- Long-term debt was $1.1B, cash was $56M and the $500M revolver was undrawn at quarter-end.
- Management expects net leverage below 1.5x by year-end 2026; FY effective tax rate is expected at 18.5%.


### Risk factors and uncertainties

- Retail partners continued conservative inventory management; Wrangler Q4 guidance assumes no improvement in POS or retail inventory levels from Q3 conditions.
- Supply-chain volatility and longer lead times delayed deliveries; higher inventory is planned through H1 2027 as conditions normalize.
- Tariffs, elevated freight and a stronger U.S. dollar pose cost and margin headwinds; current Section 301 rates imply ~$30M incremental 2027 expense if maintained.
- Helly Hansen’s sport demand remains exposed to weather; roughly 80% of second-half sport business is order-book driven, while two major selling months remained ahead.
- Lee-related stranded costs and transition expenses require mitigation; management expects to offset the structural burden within 12-18 months.


### Significant events and developments

- Lee was sold to Authentic Brands Group on Nov. 2; net proceeds were $698M, excluding tax on the gain and a potential $250M earnout tied to future Lee performance.
- Helly Hansen’s first investor day set priorities to accelerate U.S. growth, win in premium outdoor and expand workwear; most non-IT integration activities are targeted for completion by year-end.
- Helly Hansen launched a North American equity campaign and began distribution at Dick’s Sporting Goods House of Sport, expected to reach 18 stores.
- Wrangler plans additional DTC stores in Texas in 2027, including Austin, Houston and El Paso; new workwear distribution is rolling out at Lowe’s.


### Capital allocation and financing

- Q4 capital plans include $450M of share repurchases, including a $400M accelerated program, plus $280M of voluntary debt payments.
- Q3 repurchases were $20M and YTD repurchases $95M; $680M remained under the existing authorization, and the quarterly dividend was $0.54 per share.
- FY capital returns are expected to total ~$1B through repurchases, dividends and voluntary debt payments; year-end share count is expected below 50M.
- Lee sale proceeds and free cash flow fund the repurchase and debt reduction; the sale generated higher-than-expected net proceeds, partly from working-capital and closing adjustments.


### Notes to the financial statements

- Reported continuing-operations EPS included a $1.73 non-cash charge for Helly Hansen IP migration, intended to support integration synergies, a simpler operating structure and tax-platform optimization.
- Lee-related expenses previously allocated to the brand, expected at ~$55M for the full year, are now classified in continuing operations; transition-service fees and cost actions are expected to offset them over 12-18 months.
- IEEPA tariff refunds were essentially all received by quarter-end; no receivable was recorded for Section 122 tariffs, and litigation on potential recovery continues.


### Market and industry conditions

- The consumer and operating environment remained dynamic; large retail partners managed inventories conservatively, with partner inventory down at a high-single-digit rate.
- Wrangler men’s-bottoms POS moderated in Q3, while the brand continued to gain market share over the past 12 months and in September.
- Early western snowfall, House of Sport exposure and Helly Hansen’s product campaign were cited as supportive of Q4 and 2027 outdoor demand, though weather remains uncertain.
- Product costs are expected to be modestly inflationary in H1 2027 excluding tariffs, with greater inflation expected in H2; freight costs are expected to remain elevated.
