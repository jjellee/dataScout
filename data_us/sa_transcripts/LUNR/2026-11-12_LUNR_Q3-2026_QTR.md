# Intuitive Machines, Inc. — Q3 2026 (2026-11-12)

## 요약(stockanalysis 자동 생성)

### Executive summary

- Q3 revenue reached $269M, up 31% QoQ; adjusted EBITDA was positive $8M, free cash flow was $33M, and ending backlog was $1.9B.
- Strategic acquisitions expanded satellite production, communications, navigation, mission operations, and ground-network capabilities.
- Priorities include scaling execution, converting backlog into revenue and earnings, and building longer-duration infrastructure services.


### Financial highlights

- Q3 revenue was $269M; YTD revenue was $662M, with growth across satellite production, lunar delivery, lunar data services, and ground communications.
- Gross profit was $69M and gross margin 26%, versus $6M and 11% in the prior-year quarter; Q3 included ~$19M of net favorable one-time program adjustments.
- Operating loss was $34M; adjusted EBITDA was positive $8M versus negative $13M YoY and negative $14M in Q2.
- Cash was $464M at quarter-end, up $97M sequentially; operating cash flow was $66M and free cash flow was a record $33M.


### Outlook and guidance

- FY2026 revenue outlook narrowed to $920M-$950M, reflecting timing of anticipated contract modifications and awards; positive adjusted EBITDA guidance maintained.
- Backlog conversion is expected at 13%-15% in Q4 2026, 40%-45% in 2027, and the remainder thereafter.
- Q4 revenue may be level to slightly higher sequentially; 2027 financial outlook is expected with Q4 results.
- CapEx is expected to remain elevated, potentially at or slightly above Q3 levels into Q4 and Q1, as Altus 2-5 hardware is procured for a 2028 launch.


### Segment performance

- YTD revenue mix was 39% civil, 34% commercial, and 27% defense; backlog mix was 32% civil, 43% commercial, and 25% defense.
- Satellite production benefited from higher volume; six commercial IM 1300 spacecraft were in early development or production, and SiriusXM 11 was delivered and operating in orbit.
- National security awards included AMDT3, Hartford, and an orbital transfer vehicle phase; bookings also included 18 AMDT3 and two IM 300 satellites.
- Lunar portfolio includes six CLPS mission awards, with two completed; IM-3 completed integrated engine hot-fire testing and targets launch in March-April 2027.
- Altus 1 is planned for IM-3; NASA selected a booster for Altus 2-5 to launch together in 2028, with full constellation operations enabling expanded service revenue.


### Key financial ratios and metrics

- Adjusted EBITDA was positive $8M in Q3, compared with negative $13M in the prior-year quarter and negative $14M in Q2.
- Gross margin was 26%, up from 11% in the prior-year quarter.
- Backlog was $1.9B; new bookings plus assumed backlog from Goonhilly exceeded $450M.
- As of November 5, total shares outstanding were 232M: 177M Class A and 55M Class C.


### Risk factors and uncertainties

- Long-lead space-based radios and landing sensors can constrain schedules; inventory is sometimes purchased early to protect delivery timelines.
- Launch capacity after 2029 remains uncertain as Falcon 9 retirement timing and new-provider readiness evolve; boosters are secured through 2028 and government-provided launches are expected for some missions through 2030.
- Federal budgets and policy direction may change; future Golden Dome activity remains uncertain despite current AMDT3 and tracking-layer work proceeding under funding.
- A $9.5M HughesNet bankruptcy reserve was recorded in SG&A and was not excluded from adjusted EBITDA; accrued amounts were considered sufficient for receivables risk.


### Significant events and developments

- Goonhilly Earth Station and COMSAT acquisition closed August 3; integration expands U.K. ground-station coverage and deep-space mission support.
- New contract definitizations included AMDT3, Hartford, and NASA's EAGLE-VSWIR; additional awards included FALCON-RADAR and an orbital transfer vehicle phase.
- CLPS 2 is a 10-year IDIQ with an option for five additional years, valued at $10B for the base period and $5B for the option; task orders are expected to begin in 2027.
- Dr. Chris Scolese joined the board; Vantor transition services ended in October after the Lanteris systems transition was completed.


### Capital allocation and financing

- Q3 ATM proceeds were ~$97M; cumulative gross proceeds through Q3 were ~$337M at a volume-weighted average price of $23.90 per share.
- Q3 CapEx was $33M, primarily for accelerating the NSNS lunar constellation and associated ground infrastructure.
- Goonhilly acquisition cash outflow and loan repayment totaled $27M; the acquisition price was GBP 37M, with the remaining value paid in stock.
- Capital priorities target near-term program execution and long-term communications, navigation, and operations services, with spending assessed against customer demand and expected returns.


### Notes to the financial statements

- Q3 gross profit included ~$19M of net favorable one-time program adjustments, including EAC and other cost adjustments; favorable results came from satellite programs and smaller lander EAC adjustments.
- SG&A was $83M and included $30M of share-based compensation, non-recurring legal expense, and acquisition integration costs, which were excluded from adjusted EBITDA.
- Depreciation and amortization totaled $13M; R&D was $7M, driven by landing sensors, high-powered processing satellites, and larger cargo landers.


### Market and industry conditions

- Civil, commercial, and national security space each represented about one-third of the business; management cited convergence between civil and national security infrastructure demand.
- NASA's Moon Base and CLPS 2 plans point to demand for heavier lunar transport, communications, navigation, mapping, power, and sustained mission support.
- NASA's CLPS program pace has increased, with 11 of 22 CLPS 1 task orders issued since March; management expects urgency and cadence to continue or accelerate through 2027-2029.
- Orbital compute remains an early opportunity; design work is evaluating higher-volume production of high-power satellites as customer demand develops.
