# CoreWeave, Inc. — Q3 2026 (2026-11-10)

## 요약(stockanalysis 자동 생성)

### Executive summary

- Q3 revenue reached $3.7B, up 168% YoY and above guidance; adjusted operating income was $379M, with adjusted operating margin reaching 10% a quarter ahead of plan.
- Backlog rose to ~$133B, active power exceeded 1.7 GW, and demand remained well above available capacity through 2027.
- Growth is being financed through customer-backed secured debt, prepayments, operating cash flow, and disciplined CapEx, with less than $5B of incremental parent-level capital expected through 2027.


### Financial highlights

- Revenue was $3.7B, up 168% YoY and more than $1B sequentially; backlog was ~$133B, up 138% YoY, with over 50% tied to deliveries expected to begin by year-end.
- Adjusted operating income was $379M and adjusted operating margin was 10%, versus 5% in Q2 and 1% in Q1 2026.
- Adjusted EBITDA was $2.3B, up 169% YoY and 49% sequentially; adjusted EBITDA margin was 62%, and operating cash flow was $2B.
- Net loss was $709M versus $110M in Q3 2025; adjusted net loss was $529M versus $41M, while interest expense rose to $846M from $310M.
- Operating expenses were $3.5B, including $160M of stock-based compensation; Q3 CapEx was $14.5B, above guidance after infrastructure deliveries were pulled forward from Q4.


### Outlook and guidance

- FY2026 revenue guidance increased to $13B–$13.3B and adjusted operating income to $1.275B–$1.375B, a 26% midpoint increase; year-end active power remains expected above 1.85 GW.
- FY2026 CapEx guidance is $37B–$39B; Q4 interest expense is expected at $1.05B–$1.15B, and exit-2026 annualized run-rate revenue is expected at $20B–$21B.
- Adjusted net loss is expected to reduce meaningfully in 2027, with adjusted net income expected to turn positive in H1 2028 and for full-year 2028.
- If investment is limited to committed backlog, 2027 CapEx is expected to remain in line with 2026 while revenue and EBITDA grow significantly; existing customer commitments remain deliverable.
- EBITDA less interest, excluding prepayment benefits, is expected to more than double in 2027; no material risk to 2027 deliveries was anticipated at fully permitted sites.


### Segment performance

- Managed inference exceeded $250M in booked ARR; growth is constrained primarily by capacity allocated to the service.
- In Q3, 70% of new cloud customers were already Forge customers; more than half of new customers added during the year came from outside AI labs and technology.
- Forge launched in September, bringing AI development capabilities into one environment; Omni signed its first customer to run workloads on third-party-owned infrastructure.
- Customer examples include Wayve, Chan Zuckerberg Initiative, Hippocratic AI, Nissan, Aston Martin Aramco Formula One, and MasterClass across autonomous driving, biology, care, engineering, and education workloads.


### Key financial ratios and metrics

- Adjusted operating margin expanded to 10% in Q3 from 5% in Q2 and 1% in Q1 2026; adjusted EBITDA margin was 62%.
- Weighted-average cost of debt was 7.9% at Q3-end, down 250 bps YoY; Q3 interest expense was $846M.
- Q3 generated $2B of operating cash flow; cash, restricted cash, and marketable securities totaled $6.5B at September 30, with nearly $5B of undrawn committed DDTL capacity.
- More than 130,000 GPUs come off contract in 2027 and ~170,000 in 2028; at current pricing, renewals represent nearly $4B of incremental annualized revenue in 2027 and nearly $10B including 2028, without additional CapEx.


### Risk factors and uncertainties

- Forward-looking results may differ materially due to risks described in the earnings release and forthcoming Form 10-Q; statements are based on current assumptions and are not subject to an update commitment.
- Capital markets, regulatory impacts, supply constraints, and customer demand inform the pace of discretionary investment; success-based CapEx is intended to adjust spending to these conditions.
- Management cited no anticipated material regulatory impact to backlog deliveries at permitted, under-construction sites, while noting that capital and build timing remain subject to market conditions.
- Demand forecasts and industry supply timing remain uncertain; management said 2028 capacity has not yet been formally brought to market and is being held back to match client roadmaps.


### Significant events and developments

- CoreWeave Forge was introduced in September; in Q3, 70% of new cloud customers were existing Forge customers.
- The Vera Rubin architecture was brought online and delivered to Cognition for production use; Vera CPU deployments are also planned.
- International expansion added markets including Indonesia and India, with growth in Denmark and Spain; over 1.3 GW of 4.6 GW contracted power is outside the U.S.
- More than 850 MW of power was brought online year to date; active power exceeded 1.7 GW, double the year-end 2025 footprint.


### Capital allocation and financing

- The majority of remaining CapEx to deliver backlog is expected to be funded through customer-contract-matched delayed draw term loans; more than 75% of committed CapEx through 2027 is tied to investment-grade and enterprise customers.
- Nearly $5B of DDTL capacity is undrawn, and more than $4B of debt has been repaid through contract cash flows; customer prepayments and deposits of ~$9B are expected over the next three months.
- More than $3B of expected prepayments relates to re-contracting existing, fully paid-off infrastructure; this infrastructure was sold at meaningful premiums to original pricing.
- A new investment-grade DDTL was expected to launch in coming weeks, with an indicated 7%–7.5% yield and financing for more than 90% of associated CapEx; no additional parent-level unsecured debt is planned through 2027 at current yields.
- Less than $5B of incremental parent-level capital is expected through 2027; OEM financing and operating cash flow are additional funding sources, while Omni can serve demand using third-party infrastructure.


### Notes to the financial statements

- An income tax provision was recorded despite the Q3 net loss because of a valuation allowance on net deferred tax assets; absent significant discrete items or changed circumstances, the tax rate was expected to remain broadly consistent over 2026.


### Market and industry conditions

- Management described compute demand as growing faster than deliverable capacity; qualified demand was 10x available capacity through 2027, with 2028 demand already several times available capacity.
- Pricing rose across products: July SKU increases averaged ~25%, followed by another 10% later in Q3; short-term capacity was priced above $40M per MW annually, versus $20M–$25M per MW for new four- and five-year contracts.
- Q3 pricing for prior-generation Ampere and Hopper GPUs increased 30%–50% QoQ and exceeded original contract prices; renewed capacity can generate revenue without new CapEx.
- New short-duration contracts can repay hardware CapEx in 6–18 months, compared with one to two years for long-dated contracts at current pricing.
