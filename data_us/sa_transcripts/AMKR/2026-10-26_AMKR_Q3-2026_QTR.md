# Amkor Technology, Inc. — Q3 2026 (2026-10-26)

## 요약(stockanalysis 자동 생성)

### Executive summary

- Q3 revenue was $1.99B, up 5% sequentially, with record computing and automotive and industrial revenue; gross margin reached 18.3% and operating margin 11.3%.
- Advanced packaging demand, customer commitments and program ramps are strengthening visibility and supporting accelerated capacity investment.


### Financial highlights

- Gross profit was $364M; operating income was $225M; net income was $191M; EPS was $0.76.
- Gross margin rose 150 bps QoQ and 400 bps YoY, driven primarily by richer advanced-packaging mix in computing and automotive.
- EBITDA was $435M and EBITDA margin was 21.8%.


### Outlook and guidance

- Q4 revenue is forecast at $1.9B–$2.0B, gross margin at 18%–19%, net income at $160M–$185M and EPS at $0.65–$0.75.
- Q4 revenue is expected to rise low double digits sequentially and exceed 50% growth YoY; computing is expected to grow low double digits QoQ and automotive and industrial low single digits QoQ.
- Q4 communications revenue is expected to decline high single digits sequentially; consumer revenue is forecast to fall more than seasonally, into the mid-teens percent sequentially.
- FY26 CapEx estimate narrowed to ~$2.5B; CapEx steps up in 2027, with Arizona investment peaking in 2028–29 and spend expected to remain elevated before tapering.
- FY26 effective tax rate is expected in the high teens, reflecting year-to-date discrete tax benefits.


### Segment performance

- Computing revenue rose 25% QoQ to a record, supported by HDFO CPU scaling and data center programs; a hyperscaler HDFO program began ramping in Q3.
- Automotive and industrial revenue reached a record, up 7% QoQ; growth spanned ADAS, in-car computing and industrial products across multiple customers.
- Communications revenue declined 8% QoQ amid SiP migration to Vietnam, memory constraints, build-pattern changes and component shortages affecting premium smartphones and wearables.
- Consumer revenue increased 10% QoQ on IoT and home electronics demand, partly offset by component shortages affecting premium smartphones.
- Two new 2.5D networking and data center programs are expected to come online in Q4; fan-out bridge opportunity remains targeted for late 2027 into 2028.


### Key financial ratios and metrics

- Cash and short-term investments were $2.4B at Sept. 30; total liquidity was $3.4B, total debt $2.5B and debt/EBITDA 1.7x.
- Q3 operating expenses were $139M; operating income was $225M and operating margin was 11.3%.
- Q3 gross margin was 18.3%; EBITDA margin was 21.8%.


### Risk factors and uncertainties

- Q3 gross margin faced unfavorable FX and a July earthquake at the Kumamoto facility, together reducing sequential margin by ~60 bps; Japan operations returned to full capacity during Q3.
- Memory prices and availability weighed on smartphone and PC demand; component shortages are expected to continue in Q4, while FX remains an expected headwind.
- Arizona operating costs are expected to be higher due to depreciation and labor; high utilization and automation are planned to mitigate the cost difference, with no major long-term margin delta expected versus Asia.
- Elevated facility and equipment investment will increase depreciation; the 2027 margin effect remains uncertain, with product mix expected to provide a benefit.


### Significant events and developments

- Arizona phase II was announced; both campus phases are planned to exceed 90,000 sq m of clean-room space and represent an estimated $12B investment, with phase II groundbreaking expected in late 2027.
- Korea expansion includes two new Gwangju buildings for HDFO and a Songdo assembly-and-test building scheduled for year-end completion; Korea footprint is expected to grow 40% from 2025 after expansions complete in 2028.
- Vietnam build-out is progressing, with SiP migration from Korea freeing Korean clean-room space for advanced packaging; automation is central to the planned Vietnam SiP center of excellence.
- Japan’s Kumamoto facility returned to full operations within Q3 after the July earthquake.


### Capital allocation and financing

- Customer advance-payment arrangements with a dozen customers primarily support advanced-packaging capacity in Korea and Arizona; additional agreements are under discussion and payments are expected to fund a significant portion of investment.
- Capacity agreements provide greater investment visibility; management said the balance sheet is solid through 2027, while incremental debt needs in 2028 and beyond remain uncertain.
- 2027 facility spending includes up to 15% of Arizona’s $12B project, $400M–$450M in Korea and ~$50M in Vietnam; these outlined programs total more than $2B.


### Market and industry conditions

- Advanced packaging is increasingly critical to AI and high-performance computing; customers are securing capacity into 2027–28 as packaging becomes a key industry bottleneck.
- Cost pressures from substrates and materials are being managed with suppliers and generally passed through to customers; constrained capacity supports an attractive pricing environment and returns on investment.
- Data center growth is offsetting softness in PC, communications and consumer; management expects computing to grow over 30% from 2025 to 2026 and at a similar or higher rate in 2027.
- Automotive demand benefits from increasing semiconductor content, faster adoption of in-car technology and improving customer inventory conditions; industrial also showed positive momentum.
