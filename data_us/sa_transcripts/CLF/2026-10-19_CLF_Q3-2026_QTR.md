# Cleveland-Cliffs Inc. — Q3 2026 (2026-10-19)

## 요약(stockanalysis 자동 생성)

### Executive summary

- Q3 adjusted EBITDA was $612 million, more than double Q2’s $286 million and the strongest quarterly result in three years; shipments, selling prices and costs all improved.
- Strong contract renewals, rising prices and production capacity underpin an expected 2027 adjusted EBITDA path above $4 billion under market conditions similar to Q3.


### Financial highlights

- Q3 adjusted EBITDA rose $326 million sequentially, exceeding the $575 million outlook; Q4 adjusted EBITDA is forecast at $715 million.
- Q3 steel shipments were 4.2 million net tons, versus 4.0 million in Q2; automotive shipments reached their highest level since Q3 2023.
- Average net selling price was $1,177 per ton, up $53 sequentially; unit cost declined $17 per ton, better than the guided $10 reduction.
- Q3 working capital increased $160 million: inventory rose $170 million and receivables $115 million, partly offset by a $135 million increase in payables.


### Outlook and guidance

- Q4 adjusted EBITDA expected at $715 million, roughly $100 million above Q3 despite seasonal patterns and maintenance outages; ASP expected to rise another $45–$55 per ton.
- Q4 costs are expected to increase slightly due to maintenance, energy, alloys and diesel; shipments are expected to remain strong, with typical holiday and winter impacts.
- Under prevailing market conditions, 2027 adjusted EBITDA could average more than $1 billion per quarter; the forward-curve-based outlook points to the best year since 2021.
- 2027 adjusted EBITDA could exceed $4 billion, supported by H2 2026 momentum, contract resets, lower index-linked discounts and higher Stelco shipments as backlogs convert.
- 2026 CapEx remains expected at $700 million; 2027 CapEx is projected just above $1 billion, including strategic projects and Burns Harbor reline preparation.


### Segment performance

- Stelco’s hot-rolled business improved rapidly under Canada’s tariff-rate quota system; Stelco is now profitable and expected to contribute more as backlogs convert to shipments.
- Stelco’s Hamilton Galvanneal line remains challenged by imports and insufficient market volume; temporary layoffs followed, while tighter import limits could support a production restart and return of workers.
- Automotive shipments were the highest since Q3 2023 despite sequentially lower North American auto production; customer production scheduling remains uneven.
- Butler Works is adding 25% grain-oriented electrical steel production; Burns Harbor is bringing a Galvanneal line online, with hot trials in early 2027 and added output expected mid-2027.
- Spare exposed electrogalvanized capacity is being developed for automotive applications; Dearborn’s blast furnace restart depends on customers committing sufficient tonnage.


### Key financial ratios and metrics

- Q3 adjusted EBITDA margin was not stated; adjusted EBITDA was $612 million, up $326 million sequentially from Q2.
- Management expects leverage below its 2.5x target by mid-2027 and intends to continue deleveraging beyond that level.
- Q3 working capital build was $160 million; working capital is expected to be neutral in Q4.


### Risk factors and uncertainties

- Forward-looking results depend on market conditions and are subject to risks that could cause actual outcomes to differ materially.
- Global steel overcapacity and state-subsidized production remain import risks; management cited tariffs, transport costs, long lead times and supply-chain reliability as barriers to imports.
- Stelco’s Galvanneal profitability and employment remain exposed to Canadian import volumes and the effectiveness of trade measures.
- Automotive production schedules can disrupt shipment plans; Q4 also carries seasonal maintenance, energy, alloy and diesel cost pressures.
- Q4 property-sale proceeds depend on contracted closings; some buyers received extensions tied to local-community and power-supply obstacles.


### Significant events and developments

- Burns Harbor C blast furnace reline moved from mid-2027 to April 2028 after maintenance extended the campaign; deferral preserves approximately 500,000 tons of planned 2027 production.
- Middletown Works’ re-scoped modernization is a $1 billion project, including $500 million of Department of Energy support; completion is expected in Q1 2030 while production continues.
- 2027 contract renewals were reported as progressing strongly; improvements are expected to add more than $1 billion of YoY EBITDA before changes in underlying pricing indices.
- Coal contract negotiations concluded at rollover pricing for 2027, stabilizing a major raw-material cost component year over year.
- U.S. Steelworkers negotiations continued beyond the original deadline; management described discussions as professional, respectful and productive.


### Capital allocation and financing

- Property-sale proceeds and operating cash flow are earmarked for debt reduction; management expects leverage below 2.5x by mid-2027 and plans to keep deleveraging afterward.
- Debt repayment priority is the ABL, followed by bonds; management said it does not plan to issue equity.
- Q3 working capital absorbed cash, while Q4 cash generation is expected to benefit from neutral working capital and proceeds from major property sales.
- 2027 CapEx is expected to exceed $1 billion, funding announced projects and pre-work for the Burns Harbor reline; 2026 CapEx remains guided at $700 million.


### Market and industry conditions

- Domestic steel prices were described as the strongest in three years; U.S. index pricing had risen roughly $40 per ton monthly for most of 2026, with no order-book signal of a slowdown.
- U.S. steel imports remain 35%–40% below pre-Section 232 levels, according to management; customers are prioritizing reliable access to steel over price.
- Canada’s TRQ system supported a rapid hot-rolled price recovery, while Galvanneal imports remain a challenge for domestic producers.
- Management cited reshoring, infrastructure, energy, grid and defense demand as supportive of domestic steel production and investment.
