# Generate Biomedicines, Inc. — 3rd Annual Guggenheim Healthcare Innovation Conference (2026-11-09)

## 요약(stockanalysis 자동 생성)

### Lead TSLP program and clinical strategy

- Lead severe-asthma asset SOLAIRIA is in phase III; early data support potential six-month dosing and showed biomarker reductions at least comparable to tezepelumab.
- Phase II was skipped based on shared epitope, phase I biomarker results, regulatory precedent and modeling that indicated 99.9% target engagement; 300 mg was selected, with no added biomarker reduction above that dose.
- COPD is the planned next indication after asthma; phase Ib showed comparable biomarker reductions to asthma and no difference between 300 mg and 600 mg. Management favors two trials after FDA feedback.
- COPD rationale includes low-single-digit biologic use and high unmet need; cited tezepelumab phase II exacerbation reductions were mid-40% for patients with EOS >300 and 37% for EOS >150.
- EoE ranks third among potential indications; management also cited chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps, food allergy and atopic dermatitis as TSLP opportunities.


### MMAE neutralizer

- Designed to bind and clear prematurely cleaved MMAE without disrupting intact ADC, with the goal of reducing PADCEV-associated peripheral neuropathy and widening its therapeutic window.
- By end-2026, management expects to identify a dose targeting a 50% reduction in circulating MMAE; a ~40-patient expansion cohort will assess slowing or preventing progression from grade 1 to grade 2 neuropathy.
- Proof-of-concept data are expected next year; if supportive, a 100–200-patient study could assess neuropathy progression and potentially be registrational. Management said approval could precede the TSLP asset.
- Management sees potential to improve PADCEV access and treatment duration, with longer exposure potentially supporting survival; cited use was 80–90% at academic centers versus 50% in community settings.


### Armored CAR-T

- Roswell Park collaboration is testing an IL-18-armored MUC16 CAR-T for ovarian cancer, designed for potent activity; the first patient has been dosed in a phase I trial expected to enroll 10–15 patients.
- Early patient outcomes will inform next steps; management does not plan to become a long-term autologous CAR-T company and may pursue a spinout or partnership if the program succeeds.
- Management framed a potential benchmark as converting partial responses seen with T-cell engagers into complete responses with CAR-T.


### Partnerships and capital strategy

- Target-specific partnerships cover six Amgen targets, three Novartis targets and initially two Zealand Pharma targets; Zealand also extends work into obesity.
- Amgen expanded from five to six targets; a first milestone has been paid. A Novartis program has entered its preclinical pipeline, and management expects further milestones from both partnerships.
- Amgen and Novartis deals each include mid-to-high $300 million milestone potential and royalties up to double digits; both also made small equity investments.
- Cash runway extends into early 2028; financing options include partnerships, product-specific financing, debt, equity and licensing, with commercial partnerships also under consideration for the lead asset.
