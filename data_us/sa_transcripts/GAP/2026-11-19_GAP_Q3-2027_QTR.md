# The Gap, Inc. — Q3 2027 (2026-11-19)

## 요약(stockanalysis 자동 생성)

### Executive summary

- Q3 net sales grew 2% YoY, market share increased, and adjusted EPS rose 32%; results exceeded profit expectations despite mixed brand performance and unusually warm weather.
- Management lowered FY2026 revenue and profit expectations, citing softer October and early-November trends, while recent cooler weather supported a recovery in most brands.


### Financial highlights

- Net sales were $4.0B, up 2% YoY; comparable sales were flat, with higher franchise revenue accounting for much of the difference and partly pulled forward from Q4.
- Reported EPS was $0.86; adjusted EPS was $0.82, up 32% YoY. Adjusted operating margin was 9.8%, up ~130 bps YoY.
- Adjusted gross margin was 42.4%, flat YoY; adjusted merchandise margin expanded 40 bps, including tariff tailwind and higher fuel-cost headwind.
- SG&A was $1.3B, or 32.6% of sales, leveraging ~130 bps, mainly from lower incentive compensation.


### Outlook and guidance

- Q4 net sales expected flat to down 1% YoY; comparable sales expected roughly in line with net sales, with recent improvement as temperatures cooled.
- Q4 gross margin forecast at 38.0%-38.3%; merchandise margin expansion, including an expected ~150 bps tariff tailwind, partly offset by ~75 bps ROD deleverage and promotional activity.
- FY2026 revenue expected roughly flat YoY; adjusted operating margin ~7.2%, adjusted EPS $2.29-$2.34, up 8%-10% YoY, on 366M weighted-average shares.
- FY adjusted SG&A expected to leverage slightly, with ~$150M in cost savings; adjusted interest income ~ $24M and tax rate ~25%.


### Segment performance

- Old Navy comps were flat, improving from down 4% in Q2; denim, active, knits, marketing and promotions supported demand, while warm-weather discounting pressured margins.
- Gap comps rose 9% for a 12th consecutive quarter of growth; strength spanned divisions, with denim and fleece gaining share.
- Banana Republic comps declined 3%, primarily from women's assortment and weather-related softness; men's performance was stronger, and Q4 plans emphasize lighter sweaters, color and occasion dressing.
- Athleta comps fell 15%; product and customer acquisition execution fell short amid intensifying competition. A new CEO was appointed and a comprehensive business review is underway.


### Key financial ratios and metrics

- Adjusted merchandise margin expanded 40 bps in Q3; underlying expansion was 10 bps excluding tariff and fuel effects, with brand-level strengths offset by Old Navy and Athleta pressure.
- Adjusted operating margin was 9.8% in Q3, up ~130 bps YoY; ROD deleveraged 40 bps and SG&A leveraged ~130 bps.
- Year-to-date operating cash flow was $1.3B and free cash flow was $815M; quarter-end inventory at cost fell 6% YoY, while units rose 2%.


### Risk factors and uncertainties

- Warm weather delayed demand for sweaters and outerwear, drove additional promotions and contributed to a slower start to Q4; consumer pressure and sentiment remain uncertain.
- Athleta faces product relevance, customer acquisition and competitive challenges; management described the business as entering a turnaround.
- Tariff rates, fuel costs, geopolitical and weather-related supply disruptions, and promotional needs could affect margins and inventory levels.


### Significant events and developments

- Ashley Kechter, formerly Global President of Vuori and Fabletics, was named Athleta President and CEO, effective December 14; Michael Francis assumed Old Navy's leadership role earlier in November.
- Gap store remodels remain on track for ~35 locations this year; remodeled stores are outperforming their districts, and an Old Navy Herald Square flagship is planned for 2027.
- Beauty and accessories are being expanded, including nationwide Old Navy Beauty and Gap bags; these initiatives are early-stage and are not expected to contribute meaningfully to current-year financials.
- JYT partnership, Fanatics activations and other content-led campaigns are advancing the Fashiontainment platform to build customer engagement and long-term growth.


### Capital allocation and financing

- Year-to-date capital investments were $483M; FY capital expenditures are expected at ~$650M.
- Year-to-date dividends totaled $187M; a Q4 dividend of $0.175 per share was approved. More than $600M was repurchased, or 26M shares, with ~$400M remaining under authorization.
- Cash, cash equivalents and short-term investments totaled $3.0B at quarter-end; nearly $800M was returned to shareholders year to date through dividends and buybacks.


### Notes to the financial statements

- Certain Q3 and FY outlook metrics are presented on a non-GAAP basis, excluding year-to-date tariff refunds and related interest, plus a Q1 legal settlement gain and offsetting charitable donation.
- Q3 reported gross margin was 42.5% versus adjusted gross margin of 42.4%; reported EPS was $0.86 versus adjusted EPS of $0.82.


### Market and industry conditions

- U.S. apparel demand decelerated through October and into early November; management cited record warmth in October as a factor in weaker fall-category demand.
- Cooler recent weather improved trends across most of the portfolio; management expects only slight further improvement in Q4 guidance assumptions.
- Denim demand spans multiple rises, silhouettes and washes; Old Navy rose to the U.S. category's No. 2 position, while Gap improved to No. 6 from No. 9 two years earlier.
- Women's activewear competition has intensified, with newer entrants raising expectations for innovation, speed and relevance.
