# Kopin Corporation — Canaccord Genuity's 46th Annual Growth Conference (2026-08-11)

## 요약(stockanalysis 자동 생성)

### Company evolution and technology focus

- Over 40 years, the business has evolved to focus on microdisplay development, including LCD, OLED, ferroelectric liquid crystal on silicon, and MicroLEDs, with a major push into bidirectional NeuralDisplay technology.
- Developed a Neural I/O chip in partnership with Fabric.AI, enabling a 1.6 terabit AI GPU transceiver.


### MicroLED technology and production

- MicroLEDs offer up to 1.8 million nits of brightness, far surpassing OLED and LCD, and are more durable for demanding applications.
- A new production line in Massachusetts, funded by a $15 million government award, can produce up to 160,000 wafers annually, supporting millions of units.


### Defense and drone applications

- Supplies headsets for the Drone Dominance Program, with a projected need for 3 million drones and a headset-to-drone ratio of 1:10 or 1:20 depending on drone size.
- Revenue from FPV drone goggles could reach $10–$50 million next year, depending on program outcomes.


### Competitive landscape

- Main MicroLED competitors are Plessey (UK/China-owned), ams OSRAM (Germany), Mojo Vision (US), and the company itself, with a claimed 18–24 month lead over Mojo Vision.
- Recent US government mandates prohibit Chinese components in data center interconnects, creating new domestic opportunities.


### AI infrastructure and partnerships

- Partnership with Fabric.AI targets replacing copper and fiber optics in data centers with MicroLED-based optical interconnects, aiming for 1.6 terabit bandwidth per chip.
- Shared production lines for defense and AI chips lower costs and improve competitiveness, with a projected 25–35% cost advantage.


### Energy efficiency and market impact

- MicroLEDs can save 27 kW per rack per month in data centers, translating to ~$25,000 in monthly cooling savings per rack.
- Additional collaborations aim to convert waste heat to electricity, and major industry players are investing billions in US-based optical technologies.


### Government policy and security

- US government mandates all defense and AI components be sourced domestically by 2030, driven by security concerns over foreign backdoors and supply chain risks.
- Recent incidents highlight the risks of foreign firmware and reinforce the need for US-based manufacturing.


### Commercialization timeline and technical details

- AI optical interconnects are expected to be demonstrated at CES in January, with an 18-month design cycle to production, targeting mid-2028 for broader commercialization.
- Technical specs include 1.6 terabit bandwidth, NRZ keying, onboard transceivers, and a focus on short-run (up to 300 mm) applications.
