# National Energy Services Reunited Corp. — Q3 2026 (2026-11-09)

## 요약(stockanalysis 자동 생성)

### Executive summary

- Q3 revenue rose 70.9% YoY to $504.7 million despite regional disruption; multi-year outlook and 3B3 strategy reaffirmed.
- Nine-month revenue grew 70% YoY; operations reported zero evacuations and 100% customer reliability.


### Financial highlights

- Q3 revenue was $504.7 million, up $209.4 million YoY and down 3.1% QoQ, or $16 million.
- Adjusted net income was $39.6 million, up 156.8% YoY and down 12.8% QoQ; adjusted diluted EPS was $0.38.
- YTD operating cash flow was $201.5 million and free cash flow was $48.7 million; Q3 free cash flow was negative $45.9 million.


### Outlook and guidance

- FY26 revenue goal is approximately $2 billion, plus or minus; adjusted EBITDA margin expected at approximately 20%.
- FY26 CapEx forecast is $220 million-$225 million; net interest expense ~$28 million, tax rate ~22.5%, net income margin ~8%, and FCF conversion 30%-35% of adjusted EBITDA.
- Q4 FCF expected to improve on collections and working capital; management expects FY26 FCF conversion toward 35% of adjusted EBITDA.
- With regional activity flat under current conditions, 2027 revenue is expected to grow on projects and outperformance; margins should hold or improve slightly, depending on geopolitics and mix.
- 3B3 targets $3 billion annual revenue by exit 2028; North Africa was viewed as capable of 20%-30% YoY growth in 2027.


### Segment performance

- Jafurah maintained four active hydraulic fracturing fleets in Q3; stage backlog supports ongoing completion activity, with a fifth fleet delivered to Saudi Arabia pending Aramco’s start decision.
- Kuwait, Abu Dhabi, Oman and Algeria delivered strong performance; Iraq remained a key YoY offset, while September security developments affected GCC operations.
- Lithium moved from pilot to demonstration scale under a $200 million award; first 2,000 tons are due by end-2027. Water pilots are advancing in Oman.


### Key financial ratios and metrics

- Adjusted EBITDA was $95.9 million with a 19% margin; excluding $5.5 million of incremental freight and logistics costs, margin would have been approximately 20.1%.
- Gross debt was $267.3 million, net debt $160.5 million, and net leverage 0.44x adjusted EBITDA at September 30.
- Trailing 12-month return on capital employed increased to approximately 13.7%.


### Risk factors and uncertainties

- Geopolitical and security disruptions affected September GCC activity and continued to constrain Iraq; freight and logistics costs are the primary exposure and could rise if conditions materially deteriorate.
- National holidays and geopolitical delays slowed Q3 customer collections; contingency inventory increased by $45 million-$50 million over six months, tying up working capital.
- Tender timing and project starts depend on customer decisions, logistics and regional security; some awards and North African activity faced delays.


### Significant events and developments

- Completed syndicated refinancing with approximately $747 million of facilities, including $298 million term financing and $449 million uncommitted working-capital and credit facilities; added $219 million financing capacity and a lender-approved accordion up to $153 million.
- Board approved the first dividend of $0.10 per share, payable December 10 to holders of record December 3; quarterly dividends are expected subject to board approval and financial needs.
- ROYA commercialization announcements were anticipated next quarter, with deployments and contracts in Oman, Saudi Arabia and Kuwait.


### Capital allocation and financing

- Q3 CapEx was $42.7 million and YTD CapEx $152.8 million, supporting awarded contracts and readiness for growth.
- Refinancing extended maturities and strengthened financing capacity; net leverage of 0.44x remained below the long-term target of 1.0x.
- Countercyclical inventory and equipment investment supported uninterrupted service; FY26 CapEx guidance rose to $220 million-$225 million.


### Notes to the financial statements

- Adjusted EBITDA excluded $5.3 million of net charges and credits, including an impairment on a small technology investment, an expected credit-loss provision for a North African customer, and other provisions.
- Adjusted net income also excluded the $5.3 million of net charges and credits.


### Market and industry conditions

- Management expects the Middle East to invest in spare capacity, gas, storage, pipelines and export-route redundancy after disruption exposed supply risks; Saudi gas plans target an 80% increase by 2030 versus 2021.
- Saudi, Kuwait and UAE investment plans remained in place; Kuwait retained rigs, while UAE accelerated locally focused investment.
- Management expects post-conflict restart demand for intervention, workover, coiled tubing, slickline and deferred drilling, followed by multi-year capacity and infrastructure spending.
