# Corning Incorporated — Q3 2026 (2026-10-27)

## 요약(stockanalysis 자동 생성)

### Executive summary

- Q3 sales rose 19% YoY to $5.1B and core EPS grew 36% to $0.91, both above guidance; growth was led by Optical Communications.
- The quarter marked an annualized sales run rate of $20.3B, ahead of the Springboard plan’s $20B year-end 2026 target.
- The 20-30-40 plan targets $30B annualized sales by end-2028 and $40B by end-2030, with EPS expected to grow faster than sales.


### Financial highlights

- Gross margin expanded 150 bps YoY to 40.5%; operating margin rose 310 bps to 22.7%.
- Free cash flow was $661M in Q3 after more than $500M of capacity investment; 9M free cash flow reached $2.3B alongside $1.3B of CapEx.
- Q3 core EPS was $0.91; the difference from GAAP EPS principally reflected hedged-exposure adjustments and discrete tax items.


### Outlook and guidance

- Q4 sales are forecast at $5.2B-$5.3B, up ~19% YoY; EPS is expected at $0.96-$1.00, up ~36% YoY.
- At the Q4 sales midpoint, the annualized run rate would be $21B, exceeding the Springboard 2026 target one quarter after its plan upgrade.
- 2026 CapEx is planned at ~$2B, primarily to support Optical Communications growth; free cash flow is expected to increase through the planning cycle.
- Optical scale-up is expected to begin accelerating in 2028; management remained confident in that timing.


### Segment performance

- Optical Communications sales rose 44% YoY to $2.4B; net income increased 80% to $532M, a segment record for profitability.
- Optical Enterprise sales grew 84% YoY; Gen AI product sales rose more than 100%, and Enterprise reached a $6B sales run rate after quadrupling since Springboard began.
- Glass Innovations sales were $1.5B, down 3% YoY, and net income fell 10% to $328M; TV units declined 4% and notebook/handheld units about 20%.
- Automotive sales increased 4% YoY; net income was $67M, down 1%. Solar sales rose 37% to $436M, with a $7M net loss.
- Life Sciences and Emerging Growth sales increased 1% YoY; net income declined $13M YoY due to Emerging Growth investment.


### Key financial ratios and metrics

- ROIC increased 330 bps YoY to 16.7%.
- Optical net income margin improved from the mid-teens to the low 20s; management sees further opportunity, while Solar remains a modest margin drag.
- Corporate operating expenses are expected to grow more slowly than sales; variable compensation expense was above normalized levels in Q2 and Q3 and is expected to level off in Q4 before resetting in the subsequent year.


### Risk factors and uncertainties

- Optical capacity is tight as Enterprise Gen AI and Carrier customers seek newer high-density products; capacity additions are underway to address demand.
- Solar production is ramping more slowly than anticipated; productivity improvements will determine output and profitability, with the segment’s Q3 drag expected to improve gradually through 2027.
- Display glass pricing actions address a weaker yen and higher material and energy costs; increases begin in Q4 but are geared mainly toward 2027 and beyond.
- Chinese fiber exposure has been declining and is not currently a significant competitive pressure, though management is preparing for potential changes.


### Significant events and developments

- Long-term agreements with Zayo, Verizon, and AT&T support fiber and connectivity deployments for broadband and data center interconnect; management expects DCI to exceed its prior $1B end-of-decade target in timing and scale.
- By end-2026, cover glass for iPhone, including iPhone Duo, and Apple Watch sold worldwide will be produced at the Harrodsburg, Kentucky facility.
- Optical scale-up could increase fiber demand per GPU from 16 to 160 fibers if architectures shift fully from copper to optical.
- Section 232 trade action improved Solar pricing conditions and supports demand for U.S.-made products.


### Capital allocation and financing

- Customer deposits totaled $639M net for the first nine months, reflecting upfront payments less amounts returned as customers purchased committed volumes; most growth-related capital, excluding maintenance, is funded by long-term agreements.
- Customer-funded capacity additions help smooth cash flows; deposits generally arrive before capital spending and are repaid from operating cash flow as contracted volume is delivered.
- Capital allocation prioritizes high-return organic investment, a strong balance sheet, and shareholder returns; share buybacks are expected to be the primary cash-return vehicle beyond the dividend.
- Average debt maturity exceeds 20 years, with no significant maturity due in any single year; the $2B ATM equity shelf was described as housekeeping, with no current issuance intention.


### Market and industry conditions

- Q3 TV, notebook, and handheld markets were pressured by memory prices; unit volumes were down 4% for TVs and about 20% for notebooks and handhelds.
- Data center interconnect is expected to drive Carrier growth into next year and beyond; fiber-to-the-home deployments are also expected to contribute.
- Management cited continued customer demand for more optical products, faster and across broader deployments, with compute identified as a key constraint in AI infrastructure.
- Section 232 action and stronger preference for U.S.-made solar products are improving the Solar demand and pricing environment.
