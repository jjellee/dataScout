# Lightwave Logic, Inc. — Q2 2026 (2026-08-11)

## 요약(stockanalysis 자동 생성)

### Executive summary

- Transitioned further from technology development to commercialization, expanding to five Stage 3 customer programs and advancing multiple foundry engagements, with first wafer deliveries expected in August 2026.
- Focused on addressing AI infrastructure bottlenecks, particularly interconnect bandwidth, through silicon photonics and Perkinamine electro-optic polymers.
- Customer pipeline strengthened, with a new Fortune Global 500 customer moving to Stage 3, bringing total Stage 3 programs to five, and active negotiations for additional material supply and licensing agreements.


### Financial highlights

- Revenue for Q2 2026 was $33,000, up from $26,000 in Q2 2025.
- Net loss was $6.6 million ($0.04 per share), compared to $5 million ($0.04 per share) in the prior year.
- Research and development expenses rose to $3.9 million from $2.6 million year-over-year; general administrative expenses increased to $3.4 million from $2.3 million.
- Ended the quarter with $95.9 million in cash equivalents and marketable securities.
- Cash used in operating activities was $9.9 million for the first six months, up from $7.3 million in the prior year period.


### Outlook and guidance

- Expecting a busy Q4 2026 with wafer deliveries, back-end processing, and customer testing milestones.
- Volume production targeted for the second half of 2027, with operational ramp-up and Perkinamine production expansion underway.
- Confident current cash balance supports organic growth strategy and planned production ramp, assuming no major timeline changes.


### Segment performance

- Active foundry ecosystem includes GlobalFoundries, Tower Semiconductor, SilTerra, and an unnamed fourth partner, providing multiple manufacturing options and scalability.
- Three dedicated foundry runs underway, with two wafer deliveries expected in August and a third in Q4; a fourth foundry preparing for a tape-out later in the year.


### Key financial ratios and metrics

- Sequential headcount increased by 25%, focused on manufacturing scale-up, test engineering, and commercial development.
- Capital expenditures totaled $1.5 million in the first half of 2026, supporting production readiness and IT infrastructure.


### Risk factors and uncertainties

- Foundry ecosystem faces capacity constraints, process development challenges, and yield learning pressures due to strong demand and novel material integration.
- Timelines for customer programs may be affected by foundry schedules, design changes, packaging, and system testing.


### Significant events and developments

- Welcomed new CFO Fred Graffam, bringing public company finance and operational experience.
- One material supply and licensing agreement in place; active negotiations for a second agreement with the most advanced customer.
- Investments made in equipment, clean room expansion, and IT systems to enhance manufacturing and operational readiness.


### Capital allocation and financing

- No debt and $96 million in cash and marketable securities at quarter-end, following capital raises in late 2025 and early 2026.
- Capital deployed to support commercialization, manufacturing scale-up, and long-term value creation.


### Market and industry conditions

- Silicon photonics market experiencing rapid growth, with major foundries reporting significant year-over-year revenue increases driven by AI infrastructure demand.
- Industry shift toward optical networking and silicon photonics to address bandwidth and power constraints in AI clusters.

Putting more processors into an AI cluster only helps if those processors can move enormous amounts of data between each other quickly and efficiently. In simple terms, the network has to keep up with the compute. Today, it simply doesn't. As clusters become larger and denser, copper reaches its practical limits over longer distances, and power consumption becomes a bigger part of the networking problem. That is why optical networking, and silicon photonics in particular, is growing so quickly. We are seeing it in actual foundry results, not just in industry forecasts. In their latest call, GlobalFoundries said its communication infrastructure and data center business grew more than 60% year-over-year, driven in part by optical networking, and that it expects its silicon photonics revenue to more than double in 2026. This is a part of the AI infrastructure problem that Lightwave Logic is working to fix. Whether the application is scale-up, scale-out, or scale-across, the industry needs more bandwidth, less power, and smaller devices that can be easily integrated with silicon and manufactured at scale. Our Perkinamine electro-optic polymers are designed to make silicon photonics faster and more power efficient with a much smaller footprint. Just as important, they are designed to fit into the foundry and packaging infrastructure the industry already uses.
