# Space Exploration Technologies Corp. — Q3 2026 (2026-10-20)

## 요약(stockanalysis 자동 생성)

### Executive summary

- Q3 revenue rose 161% YoY to $14.6B; net income was $4.4B versus a $1.1B loss, and adjusted EBITDA increased 306% to $8.8B.
- Management described strong momentum across launch, connectivity and superintelligence, alongside major investments to expand Starship, Starlink and compute capacity.


### Financial highlights

- Revenue was $14.6B, up 161% YoY from $5.6B; net income was $4.4B, a $5.5B improvement from the prior-year net loss.
- Adjusted EBITDA reached $8.8B, up 306% YoY from $2.2B, as launch, recurring connectivity revenue and SI infrastructure drove operating leverage.
- Backlog totaled $63B at Sept. 30, including $17.5B of deferred revenue; ~$42B was expected to be recognized within 12 months.


### Outlook and guidance

- Q1 2027 revenue is forecast at $33B–$37B; management expects total-company ARR of $100B by year-end 2026, defined as December monthly revenue annualized.
- Q4 capex is expected at $35B–$40B; Q4 gross margins are expected to be more consistent with Q2 as Q3 included one-time customer-mix benefits.
- Compute capacity is expected to reach 2.5–3 GW by year-end; management expressed high confidence in adding 10 GW in 2027, with more than 20 GW possible but uncertain.
- CEO forecast annual recurring revenue of $500B–$1T by end-2027, while Starship launch cadence is expected to reach weekly in H1 2027 and twice weekly or more by year-end.
- V3 broadband satellites are expected to deploy in meaningful volume through 2027; mobile V2 launches are planned from 2027, with direct-to-cell service targeted for early 2028.


### Segment performance

- Space revenue was $1.8B, up 84% sequentially and 6% YoY, supported by favorable customer mix; Q3 included 35 launches and 12 high-profile customer missions.
- Connectivity revenue was $5.2B, up 69% YoY and 22% sequentially; consumer broadband reached 13.6M subscribers, while monthly ARPU rose 5% sequentially to $69.
- Enterprise and government connectivity revenue grew 91% YoY and 18% sequentially; aviation remained a growth driver, with Starlink contracted for roughly 12,000 aircraft.
- SI revenue reached $7.6B, increasing $5B QoQ and $6.7B YoY; solutions and infrastructure revenue was $7B, including $608M from Cursor.
- SI solutions and infrastructure revenue grew 192% QoQ excluding Cursor; the segment monetized new compute capacity and cloud services agreements.


### Key financial ratios and metrics

- Connectivity gross margin was 55%, including a one-time benefit of roughly 3 percentage points, primarily from tariff refunds; adjusted EBITDA margin expanded roughly 2 points YoY excluding that benefit.
- Connectivity gross profit doubled YoY; SI adjusted EBITDA was $5B, up $3.8B QoQ and $5.4B YoY.
- SI operating income was $431M; excluding $1.2B of Cursor-related stock-based compensation, Q3 segment operating profit was $1.6B.
- New compute deployments were generating payback in less than one year at current pricing; compute pricing was slightly above the previously discussed $30–$50 per-watt range.


### Risk factors and uncertainties

- Forward-looking results may differ materially due to risks described in the earnings release and SEC filings; management cautioned that Starship flight success is not guaranteed.
- Compute expansion depends on power, cooling, electrical equipment and infrastructure; management's >20 GW 2027 scenario remains possible but uncertain.
- Chip-fabrication targets are aggressive: a first test chip is aimed for May or June 2027, with volume production targeted for Q3 2028, subject to execution and supplier delivery.
- A potential combination with Tesla is not certain; independent committees had not agreed on terms, and shareholder approval would be required.


### Significant events and developments

- Starship Flight 14 reached orbit and deployed operational Starlink V3 satellites; Flight 15 was planned to attempt catching both stages, with return-to-launch-site licensing expected.
- New business included three Dragon human-spaceflight missions, 18 national-security launch missions, a Starlink contract for NASA's Artemis III and Starship lunar payload and Starfall missions.
- Starlink ended Q3 active in 174 markets and added 1.6M consumer subscribers; Starlink Mobile exceeded 22M monthly users, up 37% QoQ.
- FCC approval covered 15,000 mobile V2 satellites; an agreement to acquire low-band spectrum is intended to support U.S. mobile-carrier ambitions.
- Grok Bot had 800,000 paid weekly users earlier in October; enterprise customers above $1M run-rate revenue, including Cursor, rose 50% QoQ pro forma.


### Capital allocation and financing

- Q3 capex was $19.6B, primarily for SI compute expansion, Starship infrastructure and satellite production.
- Investment priorities include Raptor and vehicle production, Starship launch pads, next-generation Starlink satellites and SI compute infrastructure.
- Management cited operating cash flow, debt-market access and potential vendor/customer partnerships to fund growth while aiming to retain investment-grade status.
- Compute deployment is intended to be demand-led; capacity allocation between internal use and third-party rentals may vary, with a rough goal of 50/50.


### Notes to the financial statements

- Cursor was acquired for a stated $60B equity value; $23B was attributed to retention and replacement of outstanding awards, recognized as stock-based compensation over roughly three years.


### Market and industry conditions

- Management cited strong demand for cloud compute, with customers seeking more capacity than currently available and short-term contract renewals at higher dollars per watt.
- Management expects AI agents and connected devices to drive substantial bandwidth demand; the CEO projected demand for 1,000 times current bandwidth within three to five years.
- Aviation connectivity is still early in installation, while enterprise and government connectivity are expected to exceed consumer connectivity revenue in the future.
