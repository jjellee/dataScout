# QNT — Q3 2026 (2026-11-05)

## 요약(stockanalysis 자동 생성)

### Executive summary

- Q3 advanced hardware, error correction, Nexus software and commercialization; leadership targets universal, fully fault-tolerant computing in 2029.
- Management said execution is ahead of its plan and momentum is accelerating, while noting quarterly progress may not be linear.


### Financial highlights

- Q3 revenue was $9.1M, up 233% YoY, driven primarily by cloud platform, research and services.
- Bookings were $98M in Q3, including the Oracle deal announced last quarter; 2026 YTD bookings were approximately $104M.
- R&D expense was $97.5M, including $27M SBC; sales and marketing was $14.9M and G&A was $22.1M.
- Adjusted EBITDA loss was $80.5M vs. $45.3M a year earlier; GAAP net loss was $117.9M, including $17M attributable to Quantinuum Inc.
- Adjusted non-GAAP net loss was $75.2M; non-GAAP EPS was a loss of $0.29 per share.


### Outlook and guidance

- FY2026 revenue outlook raised to $31M–$34M from $28M–$32M; full-year bookings expected to exceed $120M.
- 2027 revenue growth is expected to exceed 100% on the higher 2026 base, supported by bookings, backlog and broad customer demand.
- Sol remains on track for commercial availability in H2 2027; integration and assembly are targeted for completion in Q1 2027.
- Apollo remains on track for 2029; management cited higher confidence in the timeline following subsystem and error-correction progress.


### Segment performance

- Cloud platform strength, alongside research and services, led Q3 revenue growth; revenue was diversified by customer type and geography, with government the largest contributor and enterprise also significant.
- Nexus had 215 organizations building on the platform, up from 180 last quarter; job submissions rose tenfold in the 12 months through August 2026 vs. the prior 12 months.
- Customer activity spans government, national labs, universities and enterprises across the U.S., Europe and Asia; management described momentum as broad-based.


### Key financial ratios and metrics

- Non-GAAP gross margin was 74.6%, excluding SBC and other non-cash charges; management attributed YoY improvement largely to revenue growth and cited a cloud-heavy mix.
- SBC totaled $37.2M in Q3; cost of sales was $3.2M, including roughly $0.7M of SBC.
- Cash and equivalents exceeded $1.9B at quarter-end, with no debt.
- October logical fidelity on Helios reached 99.9995%, equivalent to five errors per million operations.


### Risk factors and uncertainties

- Forward-looking statements cover the technology roadmap, commercial expectations and 2026–2027 outlook; actual results may differ materially.
- Revenue timing depends on contract timing and recognition; management cautioned that quarterly results may vary and progress will not be linear.
- Sol and Apollo remain subject to integration, validation and subsystem execution; management described staged de-risking as central to roadmap delivery.


### Significant events and developments

- Helios delivered back-to-back logical-fidelity records in September and October; October error suppression was reported to require 30x fewer physical qubits to reach target logical error rates.
- Sol validated its full-scale 2D trap control system; the system is expected to offer 100 logical qubits and target a logical error rate of 10^-5.
- Nexus announced a multi-generational roadmap; its September release added logical-qubit programming and error-correction tools, with hybrid workflows planned for December.
- Commercial wins included the largest cloud agreement to date, a multi-year commitment from a global financial institution, and collaborations with Johnson & Johnson and GE Vernova.
- Oracle launched a beta for hybrid quantum and AI applications; Helios deployment in Oracle Cloud Infrastructure remains on track for full deployment next year.


### Capital allocation and financing

- Technology, manufacturing supply chain and go-to-market investment is intended to sustain the technology lead; management characterized investments as calibrated and focused on high-value opportunities.
- More than $1.9B of cash and equivalents and no debt were cited as sufficient to fund the roadmap and critical capabilities toward profitability.
- Management said it would assess partnerships and other opportunities to deepen capabilities and use capital to advance the business.


### Notes to the financial statements

- The $100M CHIPS R&D award is structured as an equity contract: the U.S. government received roughly 2M shares, with cash and milestone payments, the majority upfront.
- The award does not affect the P&L; its cash was not reflected in Q3 cash and equivalents, and its balance-sheet impact may vary quarter to quarter.
- GAAP net loss separates amounts attributable to legacy shareholders who have not exchanged shares into the post-IPO public entity as non-controlling interest.


### Market and industry conditions

- Governments are treating quantum as a strategic priority, while enterprises are investing in expertise, partnerships and applications.
- Management sees quantum computing demand across medicine, materials, finance and logistics, and expects the value opportunity to expand as fault tolerance arrives.
- Q3 gross margin reflected a heavy cloud-business mix; management expects product mix and revenue levels to influence quarterly margins.
