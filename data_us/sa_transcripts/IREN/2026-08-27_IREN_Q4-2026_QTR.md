# IREN Limited — Q4 2026 (2026-08-27)

## 요약(stockanalysis 자동 생성)

### Executive summary

- Achieved $4 billion of ARR contracted for FY26, with $1 billion currently operating and the remainder expected as new capacity comes online.
- Delivered Horizon 1 to Microsoft, the first of four 50 MW deployments, with the remaining three targeting delivery in the December quarter.
- Secured major multi-cloud contracts with leading AI developers and hyperscalers, expanding and diversifying the customer base.
- Transitioned business model from mining to AI Cloud, with mining operations expected to be fully decommissioned by end of 2026.
- Demonstrated strong vertical integration across data centers, compute, and software, including Mirantis' NVIDIA-certified hypervisor status.


### Financial highlights

- FY26 Q4 revenue was $137.2 million, including $70.5 million from AI Cloud, down $7.6 million sequentially due to mining hardware decommissioning.
- Net loss of $684 million for the quarter, driven by $450.4 million in non-cash impairments and $102.1 million decrease in fair value of mining hardware.
- Exited Q4 with roughly $500 million ARR, rising to $1 billion post-Horizon 1, and targeting over $4 billion ARR by December quarter-end.
- Secured approximately $19 billion in funding over 12 months, including $16 billion from prepayments, GPU financing, and convertible notes, and $3 billion in equity.


### Outlook and guidance

- Guiding FY27 CapEx of $25–$30 billion to support contracted deployments, new data center builds, and GPU purchases for 2026–2027.
- Expecting data center and GPU CapEx to rise 15–20%, with revenue growth projected to outpace these increases.
- Targeting delivery of 300 MW IT load in 2026 and an additional 500 MW in 2027, expanding platform to 1.2 GW gross capacity.
- Ongoing expansion into Texas, British Columbia, Oklahoma, South Australia, and Spain, with a multi-gigawatt development pipeline.


### Segment performance

- AI Cloud revenue continues to grow, offsetting declines from decommissioned mining operations.
- Existing customers such as Together AI and Fireworks AI renewed and expanded contracts, demonstrating strong retention.
- New contracts signed with major AI developers and hyperscalers, including a leading frontier AI lab.


### Key financial ratios and metrics

- Three-year contract pricing up 125% since November; five-year up 70%.
- Recent three-year contracts exceed $20 million per MW IT load, with current discussions at $25 million per MW.
- Customer prepayments funding 45–55% of GPU CapEx; GPU financing covers up to 96% for investment-grade deals.
- $7.6 billion cash on balance sheet as of June 30, with $1.7 billion restricted for Microsoft GPU CapEx.


### Risk factors and uncertainties

- Revenue and CapEx guidance subject to construction schedules, GPU delivery timelines, and fundraising environment.
- Market for GPU and data center financing is evolving rapidly, requiring flexibility in capital planning.
- Transition from mining to AI Cloud involves significant non-cash impairments and operational shifts.


### Significant events and developments

- Closed $6.5 billion in GPU financing in the past three months, including $3.6 billion investment-grade at 6% and $2.8 billion non-investment grade at 9%.
- Acquired Nostrum, adding 490 MW of capacity in Spain, with Badajoz as the flagship site.
- Mirantis named inaugural NVIDIA-certified hypervisor, enhancing software layer and customer reach.
- Headcount nearly tripled in FY26, with significant C-suite hires from leading tech firms.


### Capital allocation and financing

- All data centers remain unencumbered, providing future asset-backed financing opportunities.
- Targeting an additional $8 billion in GPU financing and prepayments to support upcoming CapEx.
- Flexible approach to balancing data center build-out and compute purchases, with funding flywheel emerging from prepayments and financing.


### Market and industry conditions

- Global compute market remains tight, with strong demand and limited capacity driving up contract pricing.
- Scarcity of new grid capacity makes existing sites with spare power highly valuable.
- Regulatory and environmental compliance in Texas positions the company favorably amid new directives.
- Industry shift toward liquid-cooled GPUs and modular, adaptable data center designs.
