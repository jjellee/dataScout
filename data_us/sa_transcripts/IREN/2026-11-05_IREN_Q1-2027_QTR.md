# IREN Limited — Q1 2027 (2026-11-05)

## 요약(stockanalysis 자동 생성)

### Executive summary

- AI Cloud revenue nearly tripled QoQ to $200 million; operating ARR doubled to $1 billion, while adjusted EBITDA margin rose to 29%.
- Revenue backlog exceeded $20 billion, with two-thirds tied to investment-grade customers; more than $4 billion of contracted operating ARR is fully funded and targeted to come online in January, potentially by end-February.
- Vertically integrated operations span AI Cloud, data centers, and land and power, allowing investment and construction to be paced to demand, financing, and returns.


### Financial highlights

- September-quarter AI Cloud revenue was $200 million, up 183% QoQ; total revenue was $228 million, up 66% QoQ, including $28 million from Bitcoin mining.
- Adjusted EBITDA was $66 million, with an approximately 29% margin versus 14% in the prior quarter; net loss was $263 million.
- Gross margin before depreciation and amortization was 80%, up from 76%; AI Cloud gross margin before depreciation and amortization was 86%, flat QoQ.
- Operating expense, including SG&A and R&D, was $224 million, up $96 million QoQ; the increase included $33 million of higher cash operating expense and $63 million of stock-based compensation.


### Outlook and guidance

- More than $4 billion of operating ARR is targeted to come online in January, with timing potentially extending to end-February due to customer design changes and supplier delivery; additional capacity is expected to drive meaningful ARR growth in calendar 2027.
- For the December quarter, expect one-quarter of September-exit operating ARR of $1 billion to be recognized as revenue; incremental Horizon and Mackenzie capacity is not expected to contribute materially, mostly arriving late December or in early 2027.
- December-quarter cash operating expense is expected to increase $50 million-$60 million sequentially; Bitcoin mining hardware transition is targeted to be substantially complete by quarter-end.
- Targeting 0.8 GW of capacity by end-2027; the initial 0.3 GW is in final stages, with further construction underway across six sites.
- Planned CapEx for the remainder of FY2027 is approximately $15 billion, down from the prior estimate; Childress air-cooled deployments are expected to spread across calendar 2027.


### Segment performance

- AI Cloud: operating ARR reached $1 billion; operating capacity rose 127% QoQ to 0.1 GW, with 86% gross margin before depreciation and amortization.
- Bitcoin mining revenue fell to $28 million as hardware is decommissioned ahead of GPU installations; transition is targeted for substantial completion by the end of the December quarter.
- Data centers: $6 billion of assets today, targeted to reach $10 billion by June 2027; assets are cash-funded to date and unencumbered.
- Land and power: more than 30,000 acres across North America, Europe, and Asia Pacific, with over 5 GW of secured power and more than 35 GW in development; not all prospective capacity will convert to grid agreements.


### Key financial ratios and metrics

- Adjusted EBITDA margin was approximately 29%, compared with 14% in the prior quarter; gross margin before depreciation and amortization was 80%, versus 76%.
- AI Cloud gross margin before depreciation and amortization was 86%, consistent with the prior quarter; GPU paybacks are around 18 months or lower depending on customer prepayments.
- Illustrative one-megawatt liquid-cooled deployment: approximately $60 million CapEx; a five-year contract at $20 million per MW with prepayment of 50% of GPU CapEx yields 18-month GPU payback and around three-year payback including data-center costs; actual economics vary.


### Risk factors and uncertainties

- Horizon capacity timing depends on supplier delivery; the more than $4 billion ARR milestone may move from January to end-February if suppliers are late.
- Selective capital markets and funding costs are driving a more measured CapEx pace; growth plans may be adjusted based on financing conditions, customer requirements, and contracting.
- More than 35 GW of power is in development, but early-stage opportunities may not convert to grid connection agreements; community engagement and local conditions can affect development.
- Industry supply constraints include SSDs, RAM, and other components; supplier timing has been challenging, while GPU costs may rise as demand remains strong.


### Significant events and developments

- Horizon 1 was handed over to Microsoft with NVIDIA Exemplar Cloud status; the building and cluster were delivered in about 14 months, versus an 18-month benchmark for a 50 MW data center building alone.
- Microsoft requested network reconfiguration and upgrades for 200 MW to support latest-generation frontier models; incremental network-upgrade CapEx was described as immaterial against total cluster CapEx.
- Horizon 2 has GPUs racked and is in commissioning; Horizon 3 has GPUs on site and halls energized, while Horizon 4 GPU deliveries were expected in the coming weeks.
- Mackenzie has 100 MW of secured power, with mass grading started for new liquid-cooled data centers; construction is advancing across six sites toward the 0.8 GW expansion target.
- Modular construction methods, standardized supply chains, and off-site factory work are being explored to accelerate builds and reduce on-site labor requirements.


### Capital allocation and financing

- September 30 assets totaled $20.6 billion, including $12.3 billion of PP&E and $6 billion of unencumbered data-center assets; cash was $5.8 billion, including $2.3 billion restricted primarily for GPU deployments, and debt was $10.2 billion.
- Raised $19 billion in the 12 months to September, including $6.5 billion of GPU financing since May at 6%-9.5%; approximately $9 billion of cash and undrawn commitments were available for growth.
- Quarterly CapEx was approximately $5 billion; remaining FY2027 planned CapEx is approximately $15 billion, leaving $6 billion to fund beyond $9 billion of cash and committed sources.
- Indicative financing sources of $12 billion-$15 billion are targeted against the $6 billion of additional planned CapEx, using data-center financing, GPU financing and prepayments, and other corporate initiatives.
- Customer prepayments and GPU financing covered more than 100% of Mackenzie GPU CapEx; portfolio target is financing, including prepayments, for 90% of associated GPU CapEx.


### Market and industry conditions

- Demand was described as exceptionally strong; recent contracts included $22 million per MW on a five-year term with a customer prepayment for approximately 90% of GPU CapEx, and over $40 million per MW on a shorter-duration contract.
- Three-year pricing during the quarter was $19 million-$23 million per MW of IT load and five-year pricing approximately $17 million; older H100 capacity was re-contracted at double its original price on a multi-year term.
- GPU supply is constrained, including memory components, and hardware pricing is expected to increase; customer contract pricing adjusts through hourly rates, while payback periods have remained stable or shortened.
- Financial markets have become more selective despite strong customer demand; construction and GPU investment can be paced, with long-lead land and power development preserved as optionality.
