# Enphase Energy, Inc. — Q3 2026 (2026-10-27)

## 요약(stockanalysis 자동 생성)

### Executive summary

- Q3 revenue was $293.3M, including $85M of safe-harbor revenue; global sell-through rose 1% QoQ, below the expected ~10% increase. Channel inventory ended slightly above normal for batteries and microinverters.
- Management outlined five growth drivers for 2027 and beyond: residential batteries, Propel prepaid leases, commercial solar and storage, bidirectional EV charging, and IQ Solid-State Transformer (SST) systems for AI data centers.


### Financial highlights

- Q3 revenue was $293.3M; shipments included 1.42M microinverters and 129.9 MWh of batteries, including 655.2 MW DC of microinverters reported by the CFO.
- Non-GAAP gross margin was 45% versus 46.8% in Q2; GAAP gross margin was 43.3% versus 60%, with Q2 lifted by 15.6 percentage points from IEEPA tariff refunds.
- Non-GAAP operating income was $53.6M versus $56.7M in Q2; GAAP operating income was $6.5M versus $51.5M. Q3 operating expenses were $78.4M Non-GAAP and $120.7M GAAP.
- Non-GAAP net income was $54M, or diluted EPS of $0.41, versus $61.5M and $0.46 in Q2; GAAP net income was $23.4M, or diluted EPS of $0.17, versus $36.1M and $0.27.
- A $26.5M investment impairment reduced Q3 diluted EPS by $0.14 Non-GAAP and $0.15 GAAP, net of tax; reciprocal tariffs reduced gross margin by 1.3 percentage points.


### Outlook and guidance

- Q4 revenue guidance is $225M–$255M, including ~$51.2M of safe-harbor revenue; expected battery shipments are 100–120 MWh. Global sell-through is expected to decline ~10% QoQ, and planned shipments are ~$20M below sell-through to reduce inventory.
- Q4 GAAP gross margin is forecast at 38%–41% and Non-GAAP at 40%–43%, each including ~2 percentage points of reciprocal-tariff impact; lower sequential margins reflect mix, battery pricing adjustments, and freight costs.
- Q4 GAAP operating expenses are expected at $119M–$123M, including ~$43M of stock compensation and acquisition-related amortization; Non-GAAP operating expenses are forecast at $76M–$80M.
- 2027 framework, not formal guidance: ~$200M quarterly core revenue plus ~$200M from growth drivers; battery shipments are expected to rise from 450 MWh in 2026 to 675 MWh, commercial revenue from ~$30M to over $100M, and bidirectional EV charging could add $15M–$20M.
- Management expects seasonal Q1 softness, partly offset by G5 battery and bidirectional charger launches; commercial battery pilots are planned for Q1 and volume shipments for Q2 2027.


### Segment performance

- U.S. revenue increased ~3% QoQ and sell-through rose 5%; U.S. sell-through fell 35% YoY, in line with an estimated ~35% decline in residential solar permits. Commercial sell-through grew ~50% QoQ.
- Europe revenue declined ~11% QoQ and sell-through fell ~12%, reflecting summer seasonality and softer demand, particularly in France; ~50% of regional revenue came from storage systems.
- Netherlands battery activations rose 23% QoQ after increasing 102% in Q2 versus Q1; France battery activations fell 12% QoQ and microinverter activations fell 24%; Germany activations declined 5%.
- Propel was available in 13 states, with ~350 participating installers versus 290 in Q2 and weekly originations above 300 versus ~200; battery attachment was ~71%.
- Commercial revenue is estimated at ~$30M for 2026 and could exceed $100M in 2027; anticipated 2027 commercial battery sales are ~100 MWh, with a longer-term target of 40% share of the U.S. small-commercial market.


### Key financial ratios and metrics

- Q3 GAAP gross margin was 43.3%, operating expenses were 41.1% of revenue, and operating income was 2.2%; Non-GAAP figures were 45%, 26.7%, and 18.3%, respectively.
- Cash, cash equivalents, and marketable securities totaled $931.8M at quarter-end versus $937.7M at Q2-end; operating cash flow was ~$0.09M, capex was $11.5M, and free cash flow was negative $11.4M.
- Customer-service NPS was 79% versus 80% in Q2; average call wait time was 1.9 minutes.


### Risk factors and uncertainties

- Higher interest rates, the expiry of the 25D tax credit, unclear FEOC guidance, and weak residential demand reduced visibility; no meaningful U.S. demand recovery was expected in Q4.
- Q1 seasonality and cautious distributor inventory behavior could weigh on shipments; battery channel inventory was above normal, prompting planned Q4 shipments below sell-through.
- Reciprocal tariffs, battery price adjustments, product mix, and higher freight costs pressure near-term gross margins; management cited a long-term 40% gross-margin target while acknowledging possible short-term volatility.
- IQ SST commercialization depends on technical validation, UL compliance, customer testing, and execution; management anticipated compliance work in H1 2027, pilots in H2 2027, and commercial production near late 2027 or early 2028.


### Significant events and developments

- IQ Battery G5 pilots were planned for Q4 2026, with volume shipments in Q1 2027; the compact stackable design targets up to 30 kWh per stack and improved cost structure.
- IQ Battery C80, an 80 kWh / 40 kW U.S.-made commercial system, opened pre-orders in August; IQ9S-3P is a 548 W GaN commercial microinverter.
- Bidirectional EV charger pilots were expected in Q4 2026 and volume shipments in Q1 2027; three U.S. auto OEMs are engaged, with an additional ongoing European engagement.
- IQ SST demonstrated 13.8 kV AC-to-800 VDC conversion; 34.5 kV and megawatt-scale demonstrations were planned, with a pipeline of more than 50 potential customers and pilots targeted for 2027.
- A homeowner AI assistant was rolled out worldwide, with an installer version beginning rollout in Q3 to support fleet management.


### Capital allocation and financing

- $931.8M of cash, equivalents, and marketable securities remained at Q3-end; Q3 capex was $11.5M, and management said it would align spending to revenue while prioritizing innovation and profitability.
- Approximately $228.5M of production tax credits (PTCs) were on the balance sheet at quarter-end; $75M had been monetized under a $150M transfer agreement by September 30, followed by another $37.5M after quarter-end.
- The IRS refund of $108.3M in 2024 PTCs was expected by year-end; management anticipated sizable Q4 free cash flow from receipt of that refund.
- SolSource raised $300M to support Propel growth and was seeking further capital for expansion into additional states.


### Notes to the financial statements

- Q3 GAAP operating expenses included $39.4M of stock-based compensation and $2.9M of acquisition-related expenses and amortization; Q4 GAAP operating expense guidance includes ~$43M of stock compensation and acquisition-related amortization.
- Q3 negative free cash flow primarily reflected working-capital changes, including timing of safe-harbor revenue prepayments and PTC monetization.


### Market and industry conditions

- U.S. residential solar remained pressured by high interest rates and the post-25D transition; management said third-party permit data showed a ~35% YoY market decline in Q3 and indicated its sell-through tracked the market.
- U.S. commercial solar permits were up ~13% year to date; management cited rising electricity rates, tax credits, and new FCC restrictions on foreign-produced inverters as supportive factors.
- Europe returned to regular demand after an energy-independence-driven Q1–Q2 spike; summer seasonality and softer French demand weighed on Q3, while battery adoption opportunities were expected to improve as Dutch net metering ends in 2026.
- Management cited growing grid needs near data centers as a driver for virtual power plants and distributed batteries, with larger utility- and data-center-led opportunities developing in 2028 and beyond.
