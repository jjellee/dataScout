# Microsoft Corporation — Q1 2027 (2026-10-28)

## 요약(stockanalysis 자동 생성)

### Executive summary

- Fast start to FY27: revenue $92.3B, up 19%; Microsoft Cloud revenue $63.9B, up 30%, with growth accelerating on strong Azure and M365 Commercial Cloud demand.
- Results exceeded expectations for revenue, operating income and EPS; management cited product improvements and infrastructure efficiency as drivers of durable growth.
- Demand for AI capacity remained above supply, while engineering efficiencies helped redirect capacity to higher-demand workloads.


### Financial highlights

- Revenue was $92.3B, up 19%; gross margin dollars rose 17%, gross margin was 68%, and operating income increased 21% reported and 22% in constant currency.
- EPS was $4.89, up 18% reported and 19% in constant currency when adjusted for the impact of OpenAI investments.
- Operating expenses rose 6%; cash from operations increased 29% to $58B, while free cash flow was $18.6B, reflecting higher capital expenditures.
- Returned $10.2B to shareholders through dividends and share repurchases.


### Outlook and guidance

- Q2 revenue expected at $96B–$97.1B, growth of 18%–19%; COGS $32.7B–$32.9B and OpEx $18.5B–$18.6B, with operating margin slightly lower YoY.
- Q2 Azure revenue growth guided to 49%–50% in constant currency; customer demand expected to remain above supply, with some delayed third-party GPU contracts shifting to H2.
- Q2 M365 Commercial Cloud growth expected at ~19% in constant currency; M365 Consumer Cloud in the low teens, while Productivity and Server Licensing is expected to decline mid- to high-single digits.
- Devices and Consumer Q2 revenue expected at $15.9B–$16.4B; Windows OEM and Devices revenue expected to decline in the mid-30s.
- Q2 CapEx expected above $50B; calendar-year spend expected at ~$170B, with second-half spend rising as delayed capacity arrives. FY27 free cash flow expected to remain positive.


### Segment performance

- Agents and Infra revenue was $76.8B, up 25%; commercial RPO increased 73% to $677B, with weighted average duration of 2.3 years.
- Microsoft Cloud revenue rose 30% to $63.9B; Azure revenue increased 48%, and Foundry revenue more than tripled YoY.
- M365 Commercial Cloud growth was 17% reported and 18% in constant currency; adjusted for a prior-year revenue-recognition benefit, growth was 18% and 19%, respectively.
- Devices and Consumer revenue was $15.5B, down 3%; search and advertising ex-TAC rose 12%, Xbox revenue fell 2%, and Windows OEM revenue declined 27%.
- M365 paid commercial seats grew 5%; premium offerings and usage-based products supported ARPU, while weaker E3 and standalone SKU execution weighed on results.


### Key financial ratios and metrics

- Operating margin increased slightly YoY to 50%, supported by revenue growth and efficiency gains, partly offset by sales mix shift to Azure.
- Microsoft Cloud gross margin was 66%, down YoY; gross margin dollars rose 20% reported and 21% in constant currency.
- Microsoft Cloud operating income grew 24% reported and 25% in constant currency; operating margin eased slightly to 53%.
- Devices and Consumer operating income increased 2% reported and 3% in constant currency; operating margin rose to 35%.
- About 30% of commercial RPO is expected to be recognized as revenue over the next 12 months, up 29% YoY; the remainder increased 98%.


### Risk factors and uncertainties

- Third-party GPU capacity deliveries were delayed, shifting some supply expected in Q2 to H2; demand continued to exceed available capacity.
- Azure growth may vary with engineering-efficiency timing, capacity additions, token pricing and model mix; management described some of these drivers as less predictable.
- Commercial bookings comparisons are affected by large Azure commitments signed in the prior year, making quarterly growth less indicative of underlying sales execution.
- Windows OEM demand faced elevated channel inventory, weaker PC market demand and a strong prior-year comparison following Windows 10 end-of-support demand.
- Management said the range of potential Q2 outcomes remained wider than normal; forward-looking results are subject to risks and uncertainties.


### Significant events and developments

- Copilot received its largest update, unifying chat, co-work, autopilot and code; daily users rose nearly 50% QoQ and more than 5x YoY, with user satisfaction up nearly 20% QoQ.
- Foundry surpassed 120,000 customers and revenue more than tripled YoY; customers building multi-model agentic solutions rose 5x YoY.
- GitHub agent-generated pull requests rose 6x in six months and exceeded one-third of platform PRs; GitHub Advanced Security revenue grew over 50% YoY.
- Added 24 data centers across five continents; Copilot workload throughput rose over 50% QoQ, and Maia 200 delivers up to 1.7x more tokens per watt across Teams and Copilot experiences.
- Microsoft Purview had audited over 7.6B agent activities, up 5x QoQ; Agent 365 registry exceeded 50M agents, up over 30% QoQ.


### Capital allocation and financing

- Q1 CapEx was $45.2B, including $7.4B of finance leases; cash paid for property, plant and equipment was $39.4B.
- Roughly three-quarters of Q1 CapEx funded short-lived assets, primarily CPUs and GPUs, including higher memory and storage costs; the balance funded long-lived assets.
- Q2 CapEx is expected to exceed $50B and calendar-year spend to reach ~$170B, with H2 acceleration driven by CPUs, GPUs and delayed third-party capacity.
- Financing flexibility includes leases and access to debt markets; additional funding may be used when attractive, while management expects FY27 free cash flow to remain positive.


### Notes to the financial statements

- EPS growth was adjusted for the impact of OpenAI investments; adjusted other income and expense was -$234M in Q1.
- Finance lease principal is recognized in CapEx at lease commencement; Q1 finance leases totaled $7.4B.
- Q2 cost guidance includes an estimate for the recently announced employee voluntary exit offer; adjusted other income and expense, excluding OpenAI investment impacts, is expected at roughly -$300M.


### Market and industry conditions

- Broad-based Azure growth spanned enterprise and SMB customers, core cloud services and AI infrastructure; management described AI demand as exceeding supply.
- Enterprise AI adoption is shifting toward deployed, multi-model systems focused on workload optimization, cost, reliability, security and customer control of models and weights.
- PC market demand weakened, with elevated OEM and channel inventory; search and advertising benefited from higher volume and rates across LinkedIn, Edge and Bing.
