# Navitas Semiconductor Corporation — Q3 2026 (2026-11-03)

## 요약(stockanalysis 자동 생성)

### Executive summary

- Strategic pivot from mobile and low-end consumer to high-power markets is nearing completion; high-power growth drove Q3 revenue growth and margin expansion.
- AI infrastructure is the primary growth driver, with a grid-to-XPU power platform spanning SiC, GaN and Claros IVR technology.


### Financial highlights

- Q3 revenue was $14.0M, up 33% QoQ and 38% YoY; high-power revenue grew more than 50% QoQ and by triple digits YoY.
- Non-GAAP gross margin was 40.0%, up 50 bps QoQ and 130 bps YoY; this marked the third consecutive quarter of sequential expansion.
- Operating loss was $10.9M, versus $11.4M in Q2 and $11.5M in Q3 2025; Q3 diluted loss per share was $0.03.
- Q3 OpEx was $16.5M, versus $15.5M in Q2 and $15.4M a year earlier, reflecting targeted R&D and growth investments.


### Outlook and guidance

- Q4 revenue guidance is $16.0M ± $0.5M, up 14% sequentially; at midpoint, growth is 119% YoY and marks a fourth consecutive quarter of sequential growth.
- Q4 non-GAAP gross margin is forecast at 40.5% ± 100 bps; non-GAAP OpEx is expected at $25.0M ± $1M, including Claros costs.
- FY 2026 revenue is expected to grow 7%; high-power growth is forecast to more than offset the mobile exit.
- AI infrastructure is expected to exceed 40% of Q4 revenue and more than triple in 2027; non-AI infrastructure is modeled roughly flat in 2027.
- Management anticipates double-digit QoQ growth in 2027, with growth potentially accelerating as successive AI power-market inflections overlap; backlog extends into mid-2027.


### Segment performance

- High-power markets led Q3 growth, with triple-digit YoY expansion; by year-end, high power is expected to represent almost all revenue.
- Mobile revenue is expected to decline to low-single-digit levels by year-end and become effectively insignificant; mobile and low-end consumer are being exited.
- AI infrastructure includes data centers and supporting grid and energy infrastructure; it is expected to exceed 40% of Q4 sales and more than triple in 2027.
- Outside AI infrastructure, 2027 assumptions are broadly flat, with some industrial growth and computing flat absent greater visibility.


### Key financial ratios and metrics

- Q3 book-to-bill was 2 and has remained well above 1 for multiple quarters; backlog extends into mid-2027.
- Q3 cash and equivalents were $531.5M; pro forma for the subsequent $126M Claros cash payment, cash would have been approximately $405M.
- Q3 ended with no debt; inventory was $32.4M, including $11.2M classified as long-term.
- Management's illustrative break-even model assumes gross margin above 50% with IVR and annualized OpEx in the low $30Ms, implying break-even revenue around the low $60Ms.


### Risk factors and uncertainties

- Growth depends on execution of customer design-ins, qualifications and shipment ramps across successive AI infrastructure power architectures.
- Native 800-V products are expected in production before end-2027 and to become material in 2028; grid modernization and solid-state transformer opportunities are longer-term, primarily 2028 and beyond.
- Management cautioned that longer-range projections are harder to predict; Q3 cash also preceded the Claros payment and inventory investment is intended to secure supply and capacity.
- Management said it will continue to defend its intellectual property amid industry litigation.


### Significant events and developments

- Claros acquisition closed October 6, adding modular IVR technology for vertical and embedded power delivery; management said it doubled 2030 SAM to more than $8B.
- NVIDIA collaboration includes an 800-V-to-12-V board and an 800-V reference design for next-generation AI data centers.
- The U.S. government selected the business to develop next-generation 10-kV SiC power semiconductors; fifth-generation GaNFast shipments from the U.S. began under the GlobalFoundries partnership.
- A 1 million-unit GaN order was reported for an 800-V high-density DC/DC brick; management said program conversions were progressing faster than expected.


### Capital allocation and financing

- Q3 cash declined to $531.5M, primarily reflecting TSMC buffer inventory; the Claros cash consideration was paid after quarter-end.
- No ATM facility was used in Q3, and there was no debt; management described post-acquisition capital resources as ample.
- Planned spending includes inventory and capacity buffers, IFM initiatives, foundry capacity reservations, R&D, engineering support and operational readiness.
- IFM is designed to retain ownership of differentiating IP and selected equipment while partnering with foundries and investing to secure capacity and supply.


### Market and industry conditions

- Management described power availability as a leading constraint on AI compute; higher conversion efficiency can free power capacity for additional compute.
- The transition toward 800-V AI data-center architectures is progressing through multiple inflection points, from silicon replacement in high-power AC/DC to sidecar racks and native 800-V systems.
- Native 800-V designs target high-density racks of 500 kW and above, with programs advancing across multiple vendors and more than a dozen customer programs being sampled or developed.
- Grid modernization, battery energy storage and other power-grid applications are increasing engagement for ultra-high-voltage SiC; solid-state transformers are a longer-term opportunity.
