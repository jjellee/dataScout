# GRAIL, Inc. — Q3 2026 (2026-11-04)

## 요약(stockanalysis 자동 생성)

### Executive summary

- Favorable FDA advisory committee vote and publication of NHS-Galleri and PATHFINDER 2 results strengthened the evidence base; FDA decision expected early Q1 2027.
- Q3 test volume rose 24% YoY, while lower ASPs and increased commercial investment weighed on margins; management is prioritizing penetration ahead of potential approval.


### Financial highlights

- Q3 revenue was $37.5M, up 4% YoY; screening revenue was $37.3M, up 14%, and development services revenue was $0.2M.
- Q3 net loss was $103.6M, up 16% YoY; non-GAAP adjusted gross profit was $15.9M, down $4.1M or 20% YoY.
- Q3 adjusted EBITDA loss was $92.6M, widening by $20.9M or 29% YoY; lower ASPs accompanied volume growth.


### Outlook and guidance

- FY 2026 Galleri sales revenue growth guidance narrowed to 17%-21%; cash burn guidance remains no more than $300M.
- FDA decision expected early Q1 2027; no specific date, with active label negotiations and intended use expected to align with submitted data for adults 50+.
- Adjusted gross margin expected at 50%-60% at scale, including the Illumina royalty; scale is expected to reduce COGS by ~$100 toward ~$230 including royalty.
- 2027 ASP and volume outlook remains pending; pricing promotion began in September, with full-year ASP expected near current levels.


### Segment performance

- Galleri volumes exceeded 56,000 in Q3, up 24% YoY; more than 174,000 tests were completed in 9M, up 36% YoY.
- Q3 screening revenue increased 14% YoY; Q3 test volumes were moderately below Q2, consistent with observed seasonal softness in Q1 and Q3.
- First Epic health-system integration launched in Q3; additional integrations are planned through year-end and into 2027.
- Sales force expanded from 90 to 120 in June; the clinic channel met Q3 volume goals, with new and reactivated prescribers and repeat testing supporting performance.


### Key financial ratios and metrics

- Adjusted gross margin was 42% in Q3 2026 and 46% in 9M 2026, versus 55% and 49%, respectively, in the prior-year periods.
- Q3 ASP was ~$660 per test after September’s promotion; 9M revenue grew 24% despite ASP declines.
- Q3 adjusted EBITDA loss was $92.6M; Q3 adjusted gross profit was $15.9M.


### Risk factors and uncertainties

- Competitive activity, negative NHS-Galleri headlines around ASCO, seasonal softness and pricing promotion tempered Q3 growth and reduced ASP.
- FDA label terms and final timing remain uncertain; label negotiations are ongoing and no decision date was supplied.
- NHS reimbursement timing depends on budget and political conditions; discussions continue without a stated timetable.
- Lower ASPs and higher allocated overhead, sample-mix changes and reduced development services contributed to lower adjusted gross profit and margin.


### Significant events and developments

- FDA advisory committee voted favorably on Galleri’s PMA application, submitted in January 2026; the product is the first MCED test reviewed through the PMA pathway.
- NHS-Galleri and PATHFINDER 2 results were published in The New England Journal of Medicine and Nature Medicine.
- FDA discussions remain active and positive; intended use is expected to align with submitted evidence, with the application focused on adults 50 and older.
- UKCA approval is in place; IVDR approval submission for the rest of Europe is underway, with a potential readout in 2027.


### Capital allocation and financing

- Cash was $788.7M at quarter-end, providing runway into 2029; 2026 cash burn guidance remains capped at $300M.
- Commercial spending was pulled forward, including sales-force expansion, early promotional pricing and increased direct-to-consumer advertising.
- A $110M Samsung financing round closed in Q2 2026; commercialization discussions for Korea are ongoing.


### Notes to the financial statements

- Suspended Illumina royalty is expected to resume in late December 2026; the Q3 amount would have been $2.6M, with the first full gross-margin impact expected in Q1 2027.


### Market and industry conditions

- MCED market remains crowded, with management citing confusion among tests and emphasizing the distinction between small case-control studies and large interventional screening studies.
- NHS-Galleri results cited 4-6.5x more cancers detected through screening, nearly half Stage 1 or 2, a 20% reduction in Stage 4 incidence for a prespecified group of 12 cancers, and a 25% reduction in emergency-room cancer diagnoses.
- Management expects FDA approval to support differentiation and payer engagement; reimbursement and guidelines are expected to be stronger growth catalysts than approval alone.
