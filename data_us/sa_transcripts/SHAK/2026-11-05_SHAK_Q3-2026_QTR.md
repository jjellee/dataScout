# Shake Shack Inc. — Q3 2026 (2026-11-05)

## 요약(stockanalysis 자동 생성)

### Executive summary

- Same-Shack sales rose 2.5%, with traffic up 0.8%; this marked the 23rd consecutive quarter of positive comps and fifth consecutive quarter of positive traffic growth.
- Results combined 15.2% revenue growth, 16% adjusted EBITDA growth, and 22.5% Shack-level margins amid elevated costs and rapid unit expansion.


### Financial highlights

- Q3 revenue was $423.3 million, up 15.2% YoY; company-operated Shack sales rose 15.6% to $408 million.
- Adjusted EBITDA was $62.8 million, or 14.8% of revenue, up 16% YoY; net income attributable to Shake Shack Inc. was $16.5 million, up 31.9%.
- Restaurant-level profit was $91.7 million, or 22.5% of Shack sales; margin declined 30 bps YoY.
- Same-Shack sales growth comprised 0.8% traffic and 1.7% price mix; gross pricing was ~4% for the quarter.


### Outlook and guidance

- FY2026 outlook: low-single-digit same-Shack sales growth, 22%-23% Shack margins, and adjusted EBITDA toward the low end of the $225 million-$235 million range.
- Company-operated openings remain targeted at 60-65 in 2026; licensed openings are expected at the high end of the 40-45 range.
- Gross pricing is expected to exit 2026 at ~2%; full-year G&A is expected toward the low end of the 12%-13% of revenue target range.
- Q4 adjusted EBITDA outlook remains within the annual range despite sequentially higher marketing, team-member, and technology spending.
- A loyalty program beta may launch in Q4 2026 or Q1 2027 if it meets experience goals; no material initial comp or margin impact is expected.


### Segment performance

- Company-operated same-Shack sales rose 2.5%; average weekly sales were ~$75,000 versus $78,000 a year earlier as newer Shacks made up a larger share of the base.
- Licensing sales increased 8.3% to $236.8 million and licensing revenue rose 4.7% to $15.3 million; Middle East conflict remained a headwind, moderating versus H1.
- Sixteen licensed Shacks opened in Q3 and 32 year to date; new U.S. airport, Canada, and U.K. locations performed strongly.
- Own digital channels, now including kiosks, app, and web, represented ~60% of Shack sales; third-party delivery continued growing and was reported separately from own digital channels.


### Key financial ratios and metrics

- Food and paper costs were $115.3 million, or 28.3% of Shack sales, down 100 bps as a share of sales YoY.
- Labor and related expenses were $103.8 million, or 25.4% of Shack sales, up 50 bps as a share of sales; other operating expenses were $65.8 million, or 16.1%, up 90 bps.
- Occupancy was $31.4 million, or 7.7% of Shack sales, flat as a share of sales YoY; G&A was $45 million, or 10.6% of revenue.
- GAAP tax rate was 24%; adjusted pro forma tax rate, excluding the tax impact of equity-based compensation, was 24.2%.
- Average unit volumes remained at $4 million; new Shacks typically open at $3 million-$3.5 million in sales and high-teens margins, with a 30% year-three cash-on-cash return objective.


### Risk factors and uncertainties

- Lower-income guests remained under pressure, especially in urban markets; higher-income guest traffic offset some softness, while the consumer backdrop remained challenging.
- Rapid new-unit growth creates near-term margin and comp headwinds as new Shacks ramp; delivery-area overlap was estimated to create 20-50 bps of traffic headwind.
- Elevated beef costs, fuel and other operating costs, delivery commissions, and investments in labor could pressure margins; beef inflation moderated sequentially but remained above historical levels.
- Q4 faces a difficult comparison against prior-year value and media initiatives, including the 1-3-5 platform, alongside a dynamic macro environment.


### Significant events and developments

- Opened 21 company-operated Shacks in Q3, a quarterly record, including first locations in South Carolina; 54 opened year to date versus 30 by the same point last year.
- Project Catalyst is advancing restaurant systems, digital platforms, loyalty, data infrastructure, and AI tools to improve guest engagement, efficiency, and scalability.
- Announced entry into Brazil and plans to bring the brand to Panama, Vietnam, and U.S. regional casinos with PENN Entertainment in Q4.
- Culinary launches included the Baby Back Rib Sandwich, West Coast platform, Reese's Peanut Butter Cup Shake, and return of the Black Truffle menu; Q3 comps grew with less marketing spend YoY.


### Capital allocation and financing

- Quarter-end cash and cash equivalents were $319 million, with $250 million of convertible notes outstanding and full availability under the revolving credit facility.
- Investments in technology, supply-chain capabilities, team-member development, and restaurant maintenance support planned expansion and operating productivity.


### Market and industry conditions

- Management described an intensely competitive restaurant market with declining industry traffic and guests under pressure, reinforcing the need to earn visits through value, operations, culinary differentiation, and targeted marketing.
- Q3 blended food and paper costs declined low single digits; lower buns, chicken, dairy, and paper costs outweighed low-single-digit beef inflation. Q4 basket costs were expected to remain down low single digits.
- Supply-chain savings reflect procurement and distribution improvements as well as market prices; further opportunities remain, partially offset by new-Shack margin drag.
