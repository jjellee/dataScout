# Quantum Computing Inc. — Q3 2026 (2026-11-09)

## 요약(stockanalysis 자동 생성)

### Executive summary

- Q3 revenue reached a record $9.5 million, led primarily by expanded photonics sales; focus is shifting from technology development toward customer deployments and scalable execution.
- Priorities for the next 12–24 months: grow commercial business, scale manufacturing, and advance the quantum portfolio.


### Financial highlights

- Q3 revenue was $9.5 million versus $384,000 YoY, exceeding the $9.2 million generated in H1 2026; sales were primarily photonic products for aerospace, government, and industrial uses.
- Gross profit was $428,000 and gross margin was 4.5%; operating expenses were $29 million versus $10.5 million YoY, including approximately $1 million of acquisition-related expenses.
- Net loss was $15.1 million, or $0.07 per basic share, versus net income of $2.4 million, or $0.01 per basic share, YoY; interest and other income was $13 million versus $3.5 million.
- Net loss reflected higher operating expenses and smaller warrant-derivative mark-to-market gains, partly offset by higher revenue and interest income.


### Outlook and guidance

- No formal guidance; order and delivery timing is difficult to predict, with quarterly revenue expected to remain lumpy; management expects a strong FY finish and solid backlog entering 2027.
- Management expects most backlog to convert to revenue within 12 months, while some complex products may take longer to complete and deliver.
- Dirac-3S first customer shipment is expected in Q4; management expects customer upgrades and broader applications to expand its pipeline over time.
- Over the next 12–18 months, priorities include revenue growth, manufacturing expansion, and continued technology development; backlog growth and orders from more customers are progress indicators.


### Segment performance

- Photonics drove Q3 revenue, serving aerospace, government, and industrial applications; acquired businesses broadened commercial operations and contributed to results.
- Aerospace and government represented roughly 70% of sales, mostly through subcontracts; management expects the mix to move closer to 50/50 as commercial adoption develops.
- Sales and backlog are predominantly domestic; overseas sales remain small, while international expansion is subject to geopolitical conditions and possible export restrictions, including ITAR.


### Key financial ratios and metrics

- Gross margin was 4.5%; management sees potential for improvement through higher manufacturing utilization, operating efficiency, and scale.
- Quarter-end contract backlog was approximately $39.1 million; backlog fluctuates with contract awards and revenue conversion, and declined by roughly $2–3 million from Q2.
- At September 30, 2026, cash and cash equivalents were approximately $174.3 million, investments approximately $1.1 billion, and working capital approximately $916.9 million.
- Quarter-end total assets were approximately $1.6 billion and total liabilities were $47.7 million; liquidity is expected to fund operations for at least 12 months.


### Risk factors and uncertainties

- Revenue and contract awards are lumpy; complex, low-volume products create uncertainty in order timing, completion, delivery, and revenue recognition.
- Margins remain modest; operating costs may rise as headcount grows, though management characterized increases as gradual and Q3 OpEx as a reasonable current run-rate estimate, excluding trailing M&A costs.
- Quantum adoption will vary by market and application, and Dirac-3S performance still requires assessment across different applications.
- International sales could be complicated by geopolitical conditions and export restrictions; the impact of the political environment on the pipeline remains uncertain.


### Significant events and developments

- Dirac-3S was announced with capacity for optimization problems of up to 9,980 variables; published benchmarks compared it with Hexaly and algorithms from technical literature.
- Management said Dirac-3S outperformed on most tested problems in solution time and quality, while acknowledging some cases were comparable or less optimal; first customer shipment is expected in Q4.
- Cloud access to Dirac-3 was provided to Hamad Bin Khalifa University; other deployments and agreements include a Dirac-3 installation at a global consulting firm and a NeuraWave agreement with Planck Dynamics for defense applications.
- INmune Bio collaboration is exploring Dirac optimization, AI, and analytics for clinical research; NHanced capabilities accelerated Fab 2 versus the prior greenfield target of late 2028.
- Heterogeneously integrated photonic circuits are in development; a demonstration is targeted within 12–18 months, potentially sooner.


### Capital allocation and financing

- Approximately $70–$80 million is planned for NHanced Fab 2; equipment orders have begun, with much of the spending expected between the call and March 31, 2027.
- Several hundred million dollars have been earmarked for capital expenditure and investment; further equipment needs and acquisition opportunities remain under review.
- Acquisition decisions are intended to accelerate the quantum roadmap; management cited three acquisitions in the prior eight months and emphasized disciplined deployment and preserving working capital.
- Operations span eight states and 11 facilities; integration centers on coordinating teams, accounting, manufacturing, and resources rather than consolidating sites.


### Market and industry conditions

- Photonics can build on established optical and semiconductor manufacturing capabilities; Dirac systems operate at room temperature with relatively low power and without specialized cryogenic infrastructure.
- Current quantum demand is concentrated in government R&D and aerospace; commercial sales may grow as technologies mature and customers gain confidence in returns on investment.
- Internal analysis suggests Dirac optimization could improve data-center compute-resource utilization and enable more compute capacity without building new data centers; commercial potential remains unproven.
